Framing

Three things changed in 2026 — and only one of them is permanent

1 · Structural

AI compute became a capex line, not a project. Big-4 hyperscaler capex guided to $720–745bn for 2026, with H2 running ~$419–444bn vs ~$301bn in H1.

2 · Cyclical

Memory repriced violently. DRAM contract prices +50%+ q/q entering 2026 (some trackers +90–95%); memory is 53% of industry revenue.

3 · Policy

Tariffs and export controls became permanent features. Section 232 action signed 14 Jan 2026; Nvidia guides China data-centre compute at zero.

Why it matters: a deck that treats all three as "AI growth" will over-pay. Only #1 deserves a multiple; #2 deserves a cycle discount; #3 is a cost line and a moat.
Sources: TMT Finance (23 Sep 2026); Avnet memory outlook (9 Feb 2026); White House Section 232 action (14 Jan 2026); Nvidia FQ2-27 guidance commentary (Jul 2026).

Memory grows +249.5% in 2026 vs logic +37.3% and analog +10.2%.

Bull case

Structural shortage: DRAM contract +50%+ q/q entering 2026; tight allocation expected through 2027 as new capacity lags demand.

Bear case (the one that matters)

SK hynix itself flags an HBM price correction after 2026 as competitors add capacity. A memory rollover would remove the majority of 2026's growth.

Judgement: treat 2026 memory earnings as peak-cycle, not run-rate. We do not capitalise memory at AI multiples.

Supply

Leading-edge supply is a one-company bottleneck that is only now leaving Taiwan

Node ramp

TSMC N2 in volume ramp in 2026; N2/A16 capacity growing ~70% CAGR 2026–28; CoWoS advanced packaging >80% CAGR.

Geography

Nine new fabs/packaging plants in 2026 across Taiwan, Arizona, Japan and Germany. Kumamoto in volume since Feb 2024; Dresden in build.

Concentration

TSMC holds the large majority of ≤3nm capacity; Samsung SF2 and Intel 18A are the only credible alternatives. This is a moat and a single point of failure.

Investment read-through: scarcity of advanced nodes and packaging is the most defensible profit pool in the sector — it is priced as a toll road, and it behaves like one until the second source qualifies.
Sources: TSMC Taiwan Tech Symposium (May 2026); TSMC overseas fab ramp commentary (2026). Advanced-node share is an estimate, not re-verified.

Policy

China is no longer a revenue story for US AI chips — it is a substitution story

  • Nvidia's FQ2-27 guidance assumes zero China data-centre compute revenue; H200 approvals in early 2026 had booked no revenue.
  • Huawei China AI-chip revenue projected up to $12bn in 2026, share toward 50–60% of domestic accelerators.
  • Self-sufficiency 70% by unit volume (Jun 2026) but only ~33% by value (2024); target reset to 80% by 2030.

Mature nodes: risk, not fact

China's share of mature-node capacity goes 25% (2024) → 42% (2028E). But SMIC ran at 93.7% utilisation with record Q2-26 revenue — no 2026 oversupply evidence. The glut is a 2027–28 risk.

Direction of travel

Managed restriction plus Beijing-side substitution. Pending US bills (Chip Security, GAIN AI) would tighten, not ease. We do not underwrite a normalisation deal.

Sources: Nvidia FQ2-27 guidance commentary (Jul 2026); Goldman Sachs via Epignosis Insights (10 Oct 2026); SEMI mature-node capacity; SMIC Q2-2026 results; NBS/GACC trade data.

Valuation

The multiple has de-rated while earnings exploded — that is the whole debate

Nvidia, 18 Nov 2026

$154.30

Market cap $3.73tn · trailing P/E ~16.6x · P/S 10.4x · EV/EBITDA 15.2x · ROE 117%

What that means

Price is below the late-2025 peak zone while FQ1-27 revenue hit a record $81.6bn. The market is already discounting a slowdown — the question is whether it discounts enough.

What we could not verify

SOX index level and forward P/E; TSMC, ASML, AMD, SK hynix and Micron multiples. Forward P/E vendors disagree by 3x on Nvidia (10.8x vs 32.7x) — we will not put a single forward multiple on a slide.

Discipline: value the sector on trailing earnings and cash flow, and stress-test memory at mid-cycle prices. Do not underwrite a forward multiple we cannot source.
Sources: stockanalysis.com NVDA statistics (18 Nov 2026); financecharts (17 Nov 2026); valueinvesting.io (18 Nov 2026). Index-level valuation not verified.

Investment implications

Own the toll roads, rent the cycle, avoid the middle

Overweight

Foundry & advanced packaging and leading-edge equipment: scarcity, pricing power, and the only assets that benefit whichever AI buyer wins.

Neutral / tactical

Memory: participate while allocation stays tight, but size for a 2027 correction and take profits into strength. AI compute: hold, do not chase.

Underweight

Mature-node, analog and MCU exposed to China capacity additions (a 2027-28 risk, not yet visible) and to tariffs; China-exposed revenue lines we cannot underwrite. Sizing: no single name above 1.5% of the fund.

Portfolio construction: cap the sector at a measured weight, spread across foundry / equipment / memory / compute rather than one name, and rebalance on a 5-point drift. Review quarterly against the four triggers on the next slide.
Judgement of the fund team, based on the data in slides 3–9. Not a recommendation to any individual client.

Conclusion

Stay invested, but stop paying for the cycle as if it were the trend

  • The trend is real: AI compute has become a standing capex line for the big-4, and 2026 demand accelerated rather than digested.
  • The cycle is loud: memory is 53% of revenue and most of the growth; 2026 earnings are peak, not normal.
  • Valuation is the open question: Nvidia has de-rated to ~16.6x trailing while revenue hit records, but we could not verify index or peer multiples — so we do not claim the sector is cheap.

What we ask the committee to approve

Maintain a measured sector weight; overweight foundry, packaging and leading-edge equipment; neutral memory with a trim discipline; underweight mature-node and China-exposed lines.

Next steps

Incorporate the WSTS Autumn-26 revision (1 Dec 2026); verify SOX and peer multiples; re-run the four triggers at Q4 results.
