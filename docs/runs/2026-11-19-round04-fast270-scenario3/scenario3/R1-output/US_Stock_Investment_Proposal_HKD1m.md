## 1. What you told me, and what it means

You said: **"growth, but limit the drawdown to about -20%."** That single sentence drives the whole design.

- A 100% US-stock portfolio has historically fallen **-49% (2000–02), -57% (2007–09), -34% (2020), -25% (2022)**.
- Rule of thumb: **equity weight × ~0.5 ≈ bear-market loss**. To keep the worst case near **-20%**, equity must be about **50–55%**, not 90%.
- So this is **not** a "maximum growth" portfolio. It is a **growth portfolio with a drawdown brake**.

**Honest statement of the trade-off:** the mix below (55% equity) is stress-tested at **≈ -25% to -30%** (HKD -250,000 to -300,000) in a GFC-type event — **slightly worse than your -20% target**, because a -20% floor and meaningful growth cannot both be fully delivered. If you would rather accept **-40%/-50%** for a higher expected return, tell me and I will re-issue at ~85% equity. I will not quietly assume that.

**Assumptions I made (please correct any):** horizon **10+ years**; no near-term need for this money (property/education); you hold a **6–12 month HKD cash buffer outside this portfolio**; you can watch the account fall ~HKD 250,000–300,000 in a bad year without selling.

### Why this mix
- **VOO + VTI (37%)** = the engine. Low cost, ~3,600 companies, US earnings growth. VTI dilutes the top-10 mega-caps that make VOO risky.
- **VXUS (8%)** = insurance against a decade where US valuations mean-revert; non-US trades at a fraction of US multiples.
- **QQQM only 5%** = tech upside without letting one theme become half your money. Nasdaq-100 fell **-83%** in 2000–02 — that is why it is a satellite, not a core.
- **BND (20%)** = the brake. In 2008 and 2020 bonds rose while stocks fell; in 2022 they fell together — a cushion, not a guarantee.
- **SGOV (25%)** = a **permanent** defensive allocation, not spare cash. It earns ~Fed-funds while you wait, and it is the money you deploy *into* a -20% drawdown. **Rule:** if you spend it down to buy the dip (e.g. to 15%), you must refill it by rebalancing within 12 months — otherwise the -20% floor only holds in year one. Cost of this safety: roughly **0.6–0.8%/yr** of expected return versus a 65/35 mix.
- **5% stock sleeve** = learn with real money, capped so a mistake costs at most HKD 50,000.

## 4. How to implement (practical steps)

1. **Open a broker** with low FX spread and US market access (multi-currency account). Convert HKD→USD in **1–2 tranches** (bank spreads 0.1–0.3% vs ~0.02–0.1% at a good broker — saves HKD 1,000–3,000).
2. **Deploy in 4 tranches over ~6 months** (e.g. 40% now, 20% at months 2, 4, 6). Historically lump-sum wins ~2/3 of the time, so don't stretch beyond 12 months.
3. **Rebalance** when any sleeve drifts **>5 percentage points** from target; check **twice a year**; max 2 discretionary rebalances/yr (each round-trip costs ~0.1–0.2%).
4. **Never sell in a drawdown.** Pre-commit in writing (an Investment Policy Statement) that you will *buy* with SGOV when the S&P is down >20%, then refill SGOV.
5. **Review annually**, not daily. US market hours are 21:30–04:00 HKT — don't watch it overnight.

## 6. One decision worth making now: US-listed vs Irish-domiciled ETFs

Holding **USD 127k of US-listed ETFs** creates ~**USD 17,500–27,000** of potential US estate-tax exposure and a 30% dividend withholding drag. Note the **USD 60,000 threshold is per person and covers all US-situs assets** — US-listed ETFs, US stocks in your 5% sleeve, and cash held at a US broker.

- **(A) Simple:** all US-listed (VOO/VTI/VXUS/BND/SGOV) — cheapest, most liquid, but accept the estate-tax exposure and get advice.
- **(B) Tax-efficient:** keep **≤ USD 60,000** in US-listed ETFs and put the rest in **Irish-domiciled UCITS** equivalents (CSPX for S&P 500, VUAA, etc.) — 15% withholding, no US estate tax, slightly wider spreads.

My recommendation: **(B)** if you plan to stay in Hong Kong long term; **(A)** if simplicity matters more and you will get estate-tax advice. This is a tax question — confirm with a professional.
