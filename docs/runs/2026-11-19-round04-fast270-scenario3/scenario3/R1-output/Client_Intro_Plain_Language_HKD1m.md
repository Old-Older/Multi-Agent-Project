## 1. Why we are not putting all your money into US stocks

You are young. So the first idea is usually "all in on stocks".

But you told me one thing that changes the plan: **you want to keep losses to about -20%.**

Here is the problem. If you put 100% into US stocks, history says you will one day see this:

- 2000–2002: **-49%**
- 2007–2009: **-57%**
- 2020: **-34%**
- 2022: **-25%**

On HKD 1,000,000, -50% means **HKD 500,000 gone** — for a while. Most people cannot sleep through that. They sell at the bottom. That is how a paper loss becomes a real loss.

**Think of it like a seatbelt.** You do not wear it because you plan to crash. You wear it so one bad day does not ruin everything. Bonds and cash are your seatbelt.

So this is not a "maximum growth" plan. It is a **growth plan with a brake**.

## 3. What a bad year looks like — and why you must not sell

In a really bad year (a 2008-type event), this mix could fall about **-25% to -30%**.

In dollars: **about -HKD 250,000 to -HKD 300,000.** On paper. Not lost — just lower for a while.

A fall of -20% or more happens roughly **every 5 to 7 years**. Over 10 years, it is more likely than not that you will live through one.

**When it happens, do not sell.** Here is why:

- Selling locks in the loss. Holding does not.
- You still own the same companies. They just cost less today.
- You will have **HKD 200,000 in SGOV** — that is money to **buy more** when prices are down, not to run away with.
- Markets have always recovered from past crashes. That is history, not a promise — but it is the best guide we have.

Write this down now, while you feel calm: **"If the market falls 20%, I buy. I do not sell."**

## 4. Three things you must do

1. **Keep an emergency fund outside this portfolio.** 6 to 12 months of living costs, in HKD cash. This money is not for investing. It is why you will never be forced to sell at a bad time.
2. **Put the money in over about 6 months, not all at once.** For example 40% now, then 20% at month 2, month 4 and month 6. Prices are high today (US valuations are well above their long-run average), so spreading the entry smooths the ride.
3. **Review once a year — not once a day.** US market hours are 21:30–04:00 Hong Kong time. Do not watch it overnight. Check once a year, rebalance if anything drifts far from the plan.

## 5. Two tax points, one sentence each

- **Dividend tax:** The US takes **30%** of every dividend your US-listed ETFs pay you, because Hong Kong has no tax treaty with the US — a small yearly drag of roughly 0.3–0.5%.
- **US estate tax:** If you die holding **more than USD 60,000** of US-listed assets, the US can tax the excess at up to **40%**, and Hong Kong has no estate-tax treaty to protect you — at your size that is up to about **USD 27,000** at risk, so this needs a professional's advice.
