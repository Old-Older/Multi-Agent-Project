## What this means for positioning (5 bullets)

1. **The sector is cheap on consensus, not cheap on risk.** SOX at 13.0x forward (FactSet, 5 Nov) vs ~19.7x 10-yr average is the lowest since Jan 2019, but the discount exists because the market is discounting the 2027 EPS number itself (LSEG: +65% 2027E). Position size should be set by *confidence in the 2027 estimate*, not by the multiple — BofA's own bear case is a 35–40% EPS cut.
2. **Own the earnings, not the beta.** NVDA at 11.4x forward / 15.2x EV/EBITDA and memory (SK Hynix 3.3x, Micron 4.1x) are priced for a cycle peak; ARM (71x fwd, 158x EV/EBITDA) and AMD (33x fwd, 74x EV/EBITDA) carry the most multiple risk if 2027 estimates come down. A barbell of NVDA/TSMC/AVGO plus a small memory sleeve is more defensible than broad SOX beta.
3. **Concentration is the portfolio-level risk, not the sector-level one.** Semis were ~19.7% of the S&P 500 at the June peak (~16% now) and top-10 ≈40% of the index; a 38% semis drawdown is an index-level event. Any core S&P 500 holding is already a leveraged semis position — size the satellite sleeve net of that.
4. **History says the drawdown is large but young.** −38.5% in 136 days vs a −32%/253-day average for the last four cycles; 2000–02 (−85%, 15-yr recovery) is the tail case, 2022 (−46%, 14-month recovery) the base case. BofA's 6–9-month lead time implies a Q1–Q2 2027 trough — stage entries rather than one-shot.
5. **Watch the 2027 capex number, not the chip tape.** The single variable that resolves both the bull (Barclays US$1.3trn, Wedbush >US$1trn) and bear (BofA US$1.2trn run-rate vs US$1.555trn consensus) cases is hyperscaler 2027 capex guidance; the midterm-election-driven data-centre regulation risk (policy visible only in H2 2027 per BofA) is the second.
