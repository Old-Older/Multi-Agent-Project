## 5 bullets — investment implications

1. **TSMC remains the toll booth, but the margin debate has started.** Q3 2026 revenue +41% YoY with 66.4% gross margin (−130bp q/q on the 2nm ramp) and US$60–64bn capex shows pricing power intact; the risk is that capex (2026–28 "substantially higher") and Arizona cost dilution compress margins before 2nm/A16 revenue scales. Watch gross-margin guidance, not order news.
2. **Advanced packaging, not wafers, is the 2027 bottleneck that is easing.** CoWoS capacity ~115–140k wpm end-2026 → ~170k wpm 2027 (some estimates 200k) and the supply gap narrowing from ~20% to ~10% means packaging scarcity rents fade in 2027 — positive for accelerator OEMs (NVIDIA/AMD/ASIC designers), mildly negative for packaging-scarcity pricing at TSMC, and a tailwind for OSATs and CoWoS tool suppliers.
3. **Foundry #2 and #3 are becoming investable but not yet proven.** Samsung SF2 at ~60% yield, Taylor booked (Tesla, Qualcomm) and a Q3 2026 foundry profit turn; Intel 18A in HVM with High-NA lead but Microsoft as essentially the only named external 18A client and Foundry still losing ~US$2.1bn/quarter. Treat 2027 breakeven as a plan, not a fact; position for optionality, size for execution risk.
4. **Policy risk has shifted from "who can buy" to "where the compute sits".** The Nov 2026 BIS remote-access rule (12m TPP threshold, five-country licences, foundry/OSAT designer screening) plus the Jan 2026 Section 232 25% tariff and the Jan 2027 truce expiry create a compliance-cost layer across the AI supply chain. Allied alignment is fraying (Dutch rejection of MATCH), so single-country policy hedges are less reliable than supply-chain diversification.
5. **China self-sufficiency is real in mature nodes and constrained at the leading edge.** China holds ~30% of global mature-node capacity today with projections of up to 45% by 2027 (TrendForce/TechNews, Nov 2026; older TrendForce baseline 33% by 2027), and 2026 expansions at SMIC/Hua Hong/CXMT support domestic equipment/materials names, but HBM/memory access remains the binding constraint on Chinese AI accelerators — a structural positive for memory makers and a cap on Chinese AI compute supply.
