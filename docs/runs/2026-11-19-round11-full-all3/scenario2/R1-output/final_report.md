# Your AI, Your Campus: What HKUST's Generative-AI Rollout Actually Changes for Students and Faculty

**Standfirst:** A homegrown chatbot, a mandatory AI core and a supercomputer down the corridor — HKUST has spent the past two years wiring generative AI into everyday campus life. Here is what changed, what it means for you, and what to do about it.

## What changed

**First, the tools.** On 21–22 April 2025, HKUST became the first local university to let staff and students test-use **HKGAI V1**, Hong Kong's first homegrown large language model, developed by the Hong Kong Generative AI Research and Development Center (HKGAI) [S1][S2]. It is free and sits inside the university's one-stop **HKUST GenAI Platform**, which now offers **more than 10 large language models, including GPT-4, DALL-E, DeepSeek-R1 and HKGAI V1** [S1]. HKGAI V1 works in Cantonese, Putonghua and English [S1].

The platform is run by the Information Systems Office (ITSO) on Azure OpenAI services, conforms to HKUST data-security requirements, and encrypts and stores chat data with the University [S3]. HKUST says it exists "to ensure equitable access, including addressing geographical limitations and access to paid versus open-source tools" [S3]. A peer-reviewed HKUST survey describes it as offering "functionality akin to the public OpenAI's ChatGPT", with monthly prompt limits subsidised by the university [S4].

**Second, the rules.** HKUST lets individual instructors set their own course-level GenAI policy — a model dating back to March 2023, when the university allowed teachers to decide whether to restrict student AI use per course [S5]. What has not changed is the obligation attached to that freedom: faculty must communicate their chosen policy to students **in writing** [S3]. The Center for Education Innovation (CEI) also states that relying solely on AI-detection tools is "not the best solution" [S3].

**Third, the curriculum.** On **14 July 2026**, HKUST announced that from the **2026/27 academic year** every incoming undergraduate must complete a **six-credit AI Common Core** [S6]. Part 1 is **AISC 1000, "Foundations of AI Literacy: Human-AI Orchestration"** — a **three-credit** course, self-paced online or blended, graded DI/PA/F, aimed at first-year students; it is one half of the six credits. Part 2 is a set of **"AI Horizon"** courses taught by different schools and departments [S6][S7]. The new Common Core area is **"Human-AI Co-Creation and Data Literacy" (HAIC)**, inside the 18-credit Foundations requirement for students admitted in 2026-27 [S6][S7]. The Provost's Office says this makes HKUST "one of the first universities in the region to guarantee that all students, regardless of major" can use AI thoughtfully, built on three Senate-approved learning outcomes: Understand AI and Data; Collaborate with AI; Evaluate AI in Society [S6]. HKUST pioneered its **"Major + X"** framework in 2021/22, and more than **1,450 students** have enrolled in AI-related courses under it [S6].

## What it means for students

**You have a free, university-run front door to AI.** You do not need a personal ChatGPT subscription: the GenAI Platform is offered to all staff and students, and the university subsidises monthly prompt limits [S3][S4]. Because chat data is encrypted and stored by the University, it is the route HKUST has designed for coursework [S3].

**But the rules you are judged by are set course by course.** Your instructor decides how much AI is allowed, and must tell you in writing [S3]. Find that statement in the course outline before your first assignment, not after a submission is questioned. If it is missing, ask — the policy is the instructor's to state, not yours to guess.

**If you arrive in 2026/27, AI literacy is compulsory.** AISC 1000 is a three-credit, self-paced or blended course aimed at first-years, graded DI/PA/F [S6][S7]. Treat it as a real course with a real grade and plan it into your first-year load.

**The evidence says literacy pays off.** In the HKUST survey, 44.71% of respondents reported using ChatGPT, and AI literacy had a strong positive effect on students' ChatGPT experience (β = 0.267, p < 0.001) [S4]. Students who understand the tool get more out of it.

**If you want compute, you need a supervisor.** The HKUST SuperPOD — an NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD H800 system with 55 GPU nodes, 8 H800 GPUs each (about 440 GPUs) and 448 PFLOPS TF32 — is a University Central Research Facility open to all HKUST researchers, but accounts are applied for by faculty members as Principal Investigators [S8][S9]. Students get access through a PI's project, not individually.

## What it means for faculty

**Your course policy is still yours — but it must be in writing.** The course-level model introduced for Fall 2023-24 courses remains the framework CEI describes [S3][S5]. An unwritten policy is not a policy: put your stance in the course outline and state it in class.

**Detection is not a strategy.** CEI's own guidance says relying solely on AI-detection tools is "not the best solution" [S3]. Design assessment around what you want students to learn, not around catching them.

**Support is being built around you.** When HKGAI V1 launched, HKUST said it would provide training support for faculty to deepen their understanding of these tools [S1]. Uses cited at launch included reasoning companions, reflection aids and analytical tools [S1].

**The new core lands in your teaching load.** From 2026/27, "AI Horizon" courses are to be offered by different schools and departments [S6]. Departments must decide who teaches them and how they connect to existing majors.

**Research capacity is on campus.** HKUST opened the **Von Neumann Institute (VNI)** on **9 May 2025**, described as the first industry-focused AI research institute in the Greater Bay Area, directed by Prof. Jia Jiaya and focused on embodied intelligence, generative AI and advanced supercomputing [S10][S11].

## Why it matters

Prof. Jimmy Fung, Associate Provost (Teaching and Learning), said of the curriculum change: "AI is reshaping talent demand across industries. Today's students seek more than disciplinary knowledge; they are looking for an education that prepares them to thrive in an evolving society…" [S6] He added that the new AI core is meant to open a new model of human-AI collaborative learning, so students can not only use AI tools but combine human and machine strengths to drive innovation and problem-solving (translated from Chinese) [S6].

Provost Guo Yike, who also directs HKGAI, said at the HKGAI V1 launch that AI can improve teaching outcomes and help build a more inclusive, personalised learning environment, and that a Hong Kong-made model gives staff and students resources that fit local culture (translated from Chinese) [S1].

AI at HKUST is no longer a pilot bolted onto the edges of the curriculum. It is a platform everyone can log into, a rulebook that runs course by course, a compulsory six credits for every new undergraduate, and a supercomputer and research institute on the same campus [S1][S3][S6][S8][S10]. For students, the question is no longer whether to use AI but how to use it within the rules you are given. For faculty, it is how to teach with it — and how to say so clearly.

One caution: **HKUST has published no GenAI Platform usage statistics** — no user, prompt or licence counts appear in any HKUST release, ITSO or CEI page, or press report reviewed for this article [S1][S3][S4]. Claims about how widely the platform is used should be treated with care.

## What to do next

**Students**
1. Log in to the HKUST GenAI Platform through ITSO and check which models — including HKGAI V1 — you can access [S1][S3].
2. Before each assignment, locate your instructor's written GenAI policy in the course outline; if it is missing, ask for it in writing [S3].
3. If you are admitted in 2026/27, plan AISC 1000 (three credits) into your first year and check the HAIC requirement [S6][S7].
4. If you need serious compute, talk to a faculty PI about a SuperPOD account [S8].

**Faculty**
1. Write your course-level GenAI policy into the outline and state it in class [S3].
2. Do not rely on detection tools alone [S3].
3. Take up the training support offered around HKGAI V1 and the GenAI Platform [S1].
4. If your department will offer an "AI Horizon" course from 2026/27, start planning now [S6].
