# AI on HKUST Campuses: a new telecoms lab — and the rules that actually affect you

**A news report for HKUST students and faculty — 20 November 2026**

---

## What happened

On **21 October 2026**, at the HKUST campus, HKUST and **China Telecom Global (CTG)** signed an agreement establishing a **Joint Laboratory on AI for Telecommunications**, moving their industry–academia collaboration "into its implementation phase." The signing was followed by the laboratory's first Scientific Advisory Committee meeting.

The agreement was signed by **Chen Kai**, Executive Vice President of China Telecom Global, and **Prof. GUO Yike**, HKUST Provost, who is also **Director of the Joint Laboratory**. According to the announcement, the lab will draw on CTG's global-network business scenarios and its **AI-powered intelligent computing cloud**. Prof. Guo described it as "an important platform for HKUST to advance the deep integration of AI research with real-world industry scenarios," adding that the university would "accelerate the translation of research outcomes" and help build Hong Kong into an international innovation and technology hub.

*Sourcing note: this event is documented in a press release issued through Media OutReach Newswire on 21 October 2026. We have not located a matching page on the HKUST newsroom; the release is the primary source used here. No funding figure, compute capacity or student-participation number was disclosed, and none is reported below.*

## Why it matters to you

**1. It is the newest piece of a bigger AI build-out on your campus.** The joint lab is one of several AI announcements at HKUST this autumn: a **Center for Marine Intelligence** partnering KAUST on the "AI4Coral" project (18 November 2026, HKUST Academy of Interdisciplinary Studies). Two further items — an embodied-AI partnership with robotics firm **Unitree** feeding a developing **Future AI Lab** (signed 14 October, announced 9 November 2026) and HKUST being named anchor academic partner of **HSBC's AI institute** (4 November 2026) — rest on secondary reports only (see below). For students, the practical question is whether these partnerships turn into lab places, internships and project courses; none of the announcements we reviewed specifies student numbers.

**2. The tools you already use are the part that changes your day.** HKUST runs its own **GenAI Platform** — a ChatGPT-style service on secure cloud infrastructure where, per a peer-reviewed HKUST survey study, chat data is "protected and accessible solely by the user," with monthly prompt limits subsidised by the university. In **April 2025** HKUST announced that staff and students had free access to **HKGAI V1**, Hong Kong's first homegrown large language model, through its one-stop platform; the university's announcement presented HKUST as the first local university to open it to all staff and students.

**3. The rules are set course by course — so check your syllabus.** Under HKUST's Center for Education Innovation (CEI) guidelines on generative AI in education, each instructor sets the course-level GenAI policy and is **required to communicate the chosen policy option to students in writing** for courses from Fall 2023–24 onward. The guidelines were drawn up in consultation with stakeholders and include measures to ensure equitable access. Two consequences:

- **Two courses can have opposite rules.** Some divisions have tightened further: the Division of Public Policy states that, from **Spring Term 2025–2026**, its own AI-use restrictions apply to written assignments. (We have not verified the exact syllabus wording, so we do not quote it.)
- **Don't count on detectors.** CEI guidance states that "solely relying on AI detection tools to detect AI-generated content in students' output is not the best solution" (CEI, Guidelines and Policies) — a signal that assessment is being redesigned around disclosure and process rather than detection.

**4. Disclosure and data are the two things to get right.** The Library's AI-literacy guidance covers responsible use, intellectual property and disclosure; on data, the campus platform's user-only chat visibility is a meaningful difference from consumer chatbots, and the reason to prefer it for coursework material.

**5. Across the border, AI is becoming credit-bearing.** HKUST(GZ) announced on **22 June 2026** that from **September 2026** it offers **"Major+AI"** (21–24 extra AI credits, noted on the degree certificate) and an **"AI Minor"** (18 extra AI credits, noted on the transcript), alongside **450** mainland undergraduate places for this year's intake across 17 provinces, about half of them in Guangdong. President **Prof. Lionel Ni** said: "AI literacy is no longer optional in today's rapidly evolving job market." We found no announcement of an equivalent credit-bearing AI pathway at Clear Water Bay; that asymmetry is worth a question to your school.

**6. HKUST's model differs from its neighbours'.** According to The Standard (6 August 2023), HKU banned ChatGPT in classes in **February 2023** and **lifted the ban in early August 2023**, moving to permitted use with attribution. According to secondary reports, CUHK's principle is that AI may not be used for assessed work unless explicitly permitted. HKUST's approach — faculty discretion plus a subsidised, privacy-protected campus platform — is the most decentralised of the three, which puts the burden of knowing the rules on you.

## What we could not confirm

- No HKUST newsroom page for the 21 October 2026 joint laboratory was located; the Media OutReach release is the source.
- The Unitree and HSBC items rest on secondary reporting and have not been confirmed by an HKUST release.
- No official statement from an HKUST student union or faculty senate on generative AI was located.
- The Public Policy division's Spring 2025–2026 wording is truncated in the available source and must be checked before quotation.

## What to do next

- **Students:** read the GenAI clause in every syllabus in week one; if it is missing, ask the instructor in writing. Use the campus GenAI Platform for assessed work involving sensitive material.
- **Faculty:** state your course policy in writing and in the syllabus; CEI's guidance and the July 2026 guidebook, *Guidelines & Principles on Using Generative AI for Higher Education* (Dr. Sean McMinn and Zack Lu; HKUST login required), are the reference points.
- **Both:** watch whether the new industry labs publish student-facing opportunities — that is the open question this autumn's announcements leave unanswered.
