## 1. Social / WeChat-style post (~120 words)

**HKUST just signed its newest AI lab — here's what it means for your semester**

On 21 Oct 2026, HKUST and China Telecom Global set up a Joint Laboratory on AI for Telecommunications at the HKUST campus, signed by HKUST Provost Prof. Guo Yike (also the lab's director) and CTG Executive Vice President Chen Kai. It's the latest in this autumn's run of AI announcements, including a marine-intelligence centre with KAUST (18 Nov).

The part that touches you: your GenAI rules are set **course by course** and must be put in writing in the syllabus. Two courses can have opposite rules. Check yours in week one — and use the campus GenAI Platform, where, per an HKUST survey, chat data is accessible solely by the user.

## 2. Email newsletter blurb (3 bullets)

**Subject: New AI lab on campus — and the rules that actually affect you**

- **What happened:** HKUST and China Telecom Global signed an agreement on 21 Oct 2026 creating a Joint Laboratory on AI for Telecommunications, signed by Provost Prof. Guo Yike, who directs the lab, and CTG EVP Chen Kai. No funding or student-participation figures were disclosed.
- **Why it matters:** It is the newest of this autumn's AI announcements (KAUST marine intelligence, 18 Nov; plus Unitree and HSBC items reported second-hand). None of the announcements we reviewed specifies student places, internships or project courses — worth asking your school about.
- **What to do:** GenAI policy is set per course and must be communicated in writing in the syllabus. Read it in week one; if it's missing, ask. Use the campus GenAI Platform for sensitive coursework — an HKUST survey describes chat data as accessible solely by the user.
