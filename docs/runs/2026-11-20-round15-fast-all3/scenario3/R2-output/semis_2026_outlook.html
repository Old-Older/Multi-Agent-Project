Our judgment in one page

What we believe

  • This is an earnings cycle, not (yet) a multiple bubble. In the year to the Oct 2026 record, SOX price rose ~32% while forward estimates rose ~73% — i.e. the multiple fell while earnings rose; forward P/E ~13x vs ~24x at the 2000 peak (Bloomberg/FactSet via AInvest, 6 Nov 2026). E
  • The 2026 boom is narrow and memory-led. Memory is 54% of revenue; DRAM +246.6% and NAND +371.9% in 2026 (Gartner). Non-AI end markets are being crowded out.
  • The binding constraint is packaging, not wafers. CoWoS is fully booked; 2026 demand ≈1.0m wafer-equivalents vs ~370k in 2024 (Silicon Analysts, Jun 2026). E
  • Policy has shifted from banning sales to policing diversion. Looser on legal sales (H200 case-by-case), tighter on remote access (BIS rule, 19 Nov 2026).

What we do about it

  • Neutral total semi weight vs benchmark (within ±5pp), with the risk budget spent on sub-segment tilts rather than on beta.
  • Barbell: own the toll-booths (foundry, advanced packaging, equipment, HBM) and the recovering cyclical laggards (industrial/analog); underweight consumer-handset-exposed logic. This is a sub-segment tilt inside a neutral total weight, not a call to add sector beta.
  • Size for a −35% to −50% drawdown, not the −36% we have just lived through.
  • Hard triggers on hyperscaler capex guidance and memory contract pricing — see slide 12.
Falsifier: if Q4 2026 hyperscaler capex guidance is cut and DRAM contract prices fall two consecutive quarters, the earnings-cycle thesis fails and we cut to underweight.

The engine: hyperscaler capex still accelerating — but the strain is now financial

Spend

  • 2025 actual: ~$410–416bn combined (10-Ks). A
  • 2026 guidance, Big-4 cash capex basis: ~$720–745bn (TMT Finance, 17 Aug 2026). Higher tracker figures ($780bn+, ~$828bn) include neoclouds/SpaceX and are not comparable. E
  • Q3 2026 actual: $201.9bn in one quarter, ~2x y/y. A
  • Company 2026 guides: Amazon ~$200–220bn · Microsoft ~$190bn · Alphabet $175–185bn · Meta $125–145bn. E
  • TrendForce: CSP capex (wider universe than the Big-4) +98% in 2026, +50% in 2027. E

Strain — the early-warning lights

  • Combined FCF turned negative (~−$180bn run-rate vs +$240bn in 2024). E
  • $125bn+ raised via debt/equity in Q3 2026 to fund AI capex. E
  • ~$900bn of combined market-cap loss around Q3 prints. E
  • Ives: capex growth peaks in 2027, Big Tech FCF troughs in 1H27. E
Read: no guidance cut yet, and 2027 is still guided up. But the "digestion" evidence is financial (FCF, leverage, share prices), not order-level. That is how cycles usually start — and it is not yet proof.

Caveat: 2026 totals differ by >$100bn across trackers because of cash vs accrual capex, finance leases, and whether neoclouds/SpaceX are included. Quote a basis, not a single number.

Memory is the whole story — and the whole risk

  • Memory = 54% of 2026 revenue (27% in 2025); $837bn in 2026, >$1trn in 2027 (Gartner). E
  • Prices rising in every segment in Q4 2026; OEM memory costs +200–300% y/y (Counterpoint, 15 Oct 2026). A
  • Bernstein: DRAM/NAND +~20% in Q3 2026, tightness through 2027, normalisation possibly 2028. E
  • DRAM+NAND = 47% of CSP capex in 2026 → 68% in 2027 (TrendForce, 25 Aug 2026). E
  • HBM4 pricing estimated at 1.5–2.7x 2026 levels in 2027 — a very wide range, not a planning input; HBM bit supply +65.7% (TrendForce via AInvest, Nov 2026). E

What breaks first

  • Demand destruction: smartphone SoC shipments −21% y/y in Q3 2026 (Counterpoint, 5 Nov 2026); PC makers cutting specs and raising ASPs — units down, ASPs up.
  • Double-ordering: explicitly flagged; one scenario model puts ~42% probability on an inventory bust reversing double-orders (MacroGuru, 31 Aug 2026 — scenario, not a house forecast).
  • 2027 capacity additions (Gartner) landing into a demand base that is 54% memory by revenue.
  • GF Securities warns of a NAND surplus in 2027. E
Positioning: memory is where the earnings are and where the air pocket is. We size memory exposure to the contract-price trend, not to spot.

Structure: the chokepoint moved from wafers to packaging

  • TSMC ≈ 73% of pure-play foundry revenue (I/O Fund, Oct 2026 — third-party estimate); Q3 2026 net income NT$763bn; 3nm full; 2nm capacity to more than double by 2027. A/E
  • CoWoS fully booked; 2026 demand ≈1.0m wafer-equivalents vs ~370k in 2024 (Silicon Analysts, Jun 2026). TSMC declined to give a capacity number. E
  • Nvidia, Google, AMD, Amazon ≈ >90% of 2025 CoWoS capacity but only ~12% of advanced logic die output — extreme buyer concentration (Epoch AI via I/O Fund, Oct 2026). E
  • De-concentration under way: 5x-reticle packaging in production, 9x/14x in development; GlobalFoundries interposer deals; $100bn Arizona expansion.

Key risks — ranked by what would actually hurt us

1. AI capex digestion High

Hyperscaler capex is ~38% of revenue with >$680bn of debt financing 2024–26; combined FCF negative. Goldman draws the late-1990s telecom parallel. A 2027 capex plateau, not a crash, is enough to break the earnings case.

2. Memory air pocket High

54% of revenue in one commodity-like segment; >75% of FY2027 shipments pre-committed E — if every buyer holds a 20% cushion, the industry has built 20% excess capacity. NAND surplus flagged for 2027.

3. Policy cliff-edges High

Affiliates Rule expires 10 Jan 2027; Section 232 Phase 2 scope/rate unknown; China rare-earth retaliation pending. Binary, unhedgeable at the stock level.

4. Circular financing & accounting quality Medium

$3trn+ data-centre lease commitments E; Nvidia $518bn of financial commitments and 64 days sales in receivables E; depreciation drag (Alphabet ~$6.4bn/yr). Useful-life assumptions flatter near-term EPS.

5. Concentration & crowding Medium

Nvidia ~6% of the S&P 500 after the fall (was ~9%); $7.4bn of AI-linked insider sales May–Oct 2026; leadership change at Nvidia (CEO resigned 12 Nov 2026, stock −25% in November — single-sourced, causation unverified).

6. Valuation & crowding Medium

Even at ~13x forward, the sector is priced for continued earnings growth; a multiple re-rating to the 2000-era ~24x is not the risk — an earnings cut is. Crowding: $7.4bn of AI-linked insider sales May–Oct 2026; Nvidia ~6% of the S&P 500. E

7. Data quality Low-Medium

2026 industry forecasts span $1.51–1.68trn and were revised up 25% in five months; several figures in this deck are single-sourced (see slide 14). Consensus built on upward revisions is fragile in both directions.

Glossary — terms used in this deck

  • SOX / SMH — Philadelphia Semiconductor Index / the main US-listed semi ETF tracking it.
  • CoWoS — TSMC's chip-on-wafer-on-substrate advanced packaging; the step that puts HBM next to a GPU die. Reticle = the mask field size; 5x/9x reticle = how much silicon one package can hold.
  • HBM — high-bandwidth memory, stacked DRAM sold with AI accelerators. DRAM/NAND — working memory / storage memory.
  • Foundry — contract chip manufacturer. Semicap — semiconductor capital equipment.
  • Hyperscaler / CSP — the large cloud operators (here: Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta). CSP is the wider universe.
  • FCF — free cash flow (operating cash flow less capex).
  • SoC — system-on-chip, the main processor in a phone.
  • Barbell — owning both the most defensive and the most cyclical parts, little in the middle. Air pocket — a sudden gap down in price/orders. Digestion — customers absorbing capacity already bought, so new orders slow.
  • Falsifier — the observation that would prove our judgment wrong.
