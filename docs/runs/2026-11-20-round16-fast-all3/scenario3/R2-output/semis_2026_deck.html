Semiconductors: what the 2027–2030 cycle looks like from here

A sector-level view for the investment committee: where the money is being made, what is already in the price, three scenarios for 2027–2030, and the tilt we propose.

Prepared by the fund manager · Research cut-off 20 November 2026 · All figures sourced and dated on the slides · Sector-level only: no single-stock recommendations, no price targets.

01Executive summary — five judgments

1. 2026 was a memory-price event, not a broad boom

Global semi revenue rose ~92–108% to $1.56–1.66trn in 2026, but memory alone was 54% of the market (27% in 2025). Non-memory grew a healthy but ordinary ~22%. Extrapolating 2026 growth is the single biggest analytical error available to us.

2. The AI capex cycle is intact but has changed character

No order cuts are visible as of November 2026. The constraint has migrated from demand to financing: only one of the six big spenders is projected free-cash-flow positive in 2027 unverified — aggregator, and NVIDIA is now underwriting customers' buildouts ($118bn of guarantees).

3. Valuation is no longer the problem; durability is

After a −38.5% drawdown from the June 2026 peak, the SOX trades at 13x forward earnings — a discount to the S&P 500 at 17.8x. The multiple halved because earnings estimates rose ~155%, not because prices fell. The question is whether that EPS ramp holds.

4. The next leg is breadth, not another AI multiple

Analog/MCU/industrial has turned up with pricing power and lead times stretching (NXP: ~80% of products >16 weeks vs ~25% a year ago). Equipment demand has rotated from China to global capacity: 2027 WFE guided >$200bn vs ~$150bn in 2026.

5. Our proposal: own the scarcity, underweight the price-takers, keep dry powder

Overweight leading-edge foundry, advanced packaging, HBM and WFE; market-weight AI accelerators; underweight commodity memory and China-exposed mature-node; hold a cash/short-duration buffer sized to the 2028 memory turn. Full tilt on slide 9, including the 10–15% dry powder and the add-at-−20%/−35% rule.

The demand mix is broadening beyond the four hyperscalers

  • Neoclouds and enterprises are the fastest-growing slice (+195%), which lengthens the demand runway but also raises counterparty quality questions.
  • AMD data centre $8.5bn (~2x y/y); Q4 guided ~$17bn; management targets ~$92bn of 2027 revenue (+~80%).
  • TSMC HPC = 68% of Q3 2026 revenue; FY26 revenue +~44%; Q4 guided $52.3–54.3bn.
  • China is being guided to zero in NVIDIA's Q4 data-centre compute outlook — a policy cut, not a demand cut.

Three things that make this cycle different from 2021

  • Financing, not demand, is the binding constraint. Only one of the six big spenders is projected FCF-positive in 2027; Big Tech FCF troughs in 1H2027 unverified — aggregator, not primary guidance.
  • Vendor balance sheets carry the risk. NVIDIA supply commitments $390bn (from $279bn), guarantees $118bn — including $105bn of credit support for one data-centre project.
  • Supply is genuinely sold out. HBM4 2027 supply is sold out; CoWoS gap narrows only from ~20% to ~10% by end-2026.

HBM is the scarce asset, not DRAM

  • HBM4 2027 pricing $3.00–5.50/Gb vs $2.05/Gb in 2026 — 1.5x to 2.7x, and 2027 supply is sold out.
  • HBM rises to ~30% of DRAM wafer input in 2027 (23% in 2026) and consumes 3–4x the wafers per bit — it crowds out commodity DRAM.
  • 2027 bit supply: HBM +65.7%, PC DRAM −7.9%.

Our read

Memory is where the 2026 earnings surprise came from and where the 2028 risk sits. The HBM franchise is a structural, contracted business (long-term agreements rising, sold-out 2027); commodity DRAM/NAND is a price-taker whose peak margins are already in the numbers.

We therefore treat memory as two exposures, not one: own HBM-linked scarcity, fade commodity memory into 2027.

What would change our mind

  • HBM4 qualification slippage at a second supplier → pricing power extends into 2028.
  • CXMT capacity growth above ~20% → commodity DRAM turns earlier than 2028.
  • Hyperscaler capex cut → HBM bit demand falls before the price cycle does.
Sources: TrendForce 18 Nov 2026 & 4 Nov 2026; Gartner Aug 20265 / 14

Why this matters for the portfolio

  • China exposure is falling structurally at every major toolmaker, while absolute China dollars still grow. The de-rating risk from China is largely behind the sector; the growth is now US, Europe and Taiwan.
  • ASML demand exceeds supply through at least 2028, with customers seeking orders into 2029–2030 — the longest visibility in the chain.
  • Packaging, not wafers, is the bottleneck. CoWoS pricing power persists (10–20% increases being weighed) even as the gap narrows.
  • Risk: equipment is the most cyclical part of the chain. A 2028 capex pause hits WFE with a 2–4 quarter lag and full amplitude.

Why we think this is durable — and fragile

  • Durable: customers are not hoarding as in 2021; TI describes the cycle as early-stage; the tightness is being created by AI absorbing leading-edge and packaging capacity, not by end-demand euphoria.
  • Fragile: that same mechanism means an AI capex pause would release capacity into analog and hit pricing within two to three quarters.

09Portfolio construction: a barbell, not a bet

Core (60–70% of the semi sleeve)

  • Broad index exposure (SOX-type) as the default — forward P/E 13x vs S&P 500 17.8x is a rare entry point.
  • Add foundry/packaging and WFE as satellite overweights: they are paid on capacity, not on model hype.
  • Cap any single issuer at 1.5x its index weight; the index already gives 12% to the largest holding.

Satellite (30–40%)

  • Analog/MCU recovery — the part of the cycle that has not yet been priced for a boom.
  • Memory: tactical, with a pre-agreed exit rule tied to DRAM q/q price momentum (exit when q/q gains fall below +5%).
  • Hold 10–15% dry powder for a policy shock (10 Jan 2027) or a capex-digestion drawdown — enough to fund both add levels below.

Rules we would write down now

  1. Position limit: no more than 1.5x index weight in any single issuer.
  2. Drawdown plan: the SOX fell 38.5% in 136 days in 2026 and 46.5% over a 10-month bear market in 2022 — assume a 40–50% drawdown is possible and pre-commit to adding at −20% and −35% from the peak (a −40% trigger would not have fired in 2026).
  3. Trigger to cut: two consecutive quarters of hyperscaler capex guidance cuts, or DRAM contract prices turning negative q/q.
  4. Trigger to add: WFE 2027 orders confirmed above $200bn with China share stabilised.

11Key risks: what would make this wrong

  1. AI capex digestion. The single largest demand driver. Financing, not demand, is now the constraint; Big Tech FCF troughs 1H2027 (unverified aggregator estimate). Early warnings: vendor supply commitments and guarantees rising faster than revenue, and neocloud order deferrals.
  2. Memory cycle reversal. Memory is 54% of 2026 industry revenue; TrendForce dates the turn to 2028. A faster turn hits index EPS directly. We have not sized the amplitude of a memory downturn — that is a gap in this deck.
  3. Policy shock. BIS Affiliates Rule re-imposition on 10 Jan 2027 (stayed, not repealed); MATCH Act pending; China's October-2025 rare-earth controls suspended for one year, with the April-2025 controls' status ambiguous.
  4. Concentration. Top 10 = 60.5% of the SOXQ proxy; a single customer or single product slip moves the whole index. Hyperscaler share of the largest vendor's revenue was not verified.
  5. Neocloud counterparty risk. The fastest-growing demand slice (+195% y/y) is the least credit-tested; a single large default would hit both demand and vendor guarantees.
  6. Valuation is cheap for a reason. 13x forward is a discount to the S&P 500 — the market is already discounting the durability of the +155% EPS ramp. Test: if 2027 EPS estimates are cut by more than 10%, the multiple is not cheap.
  7. Mature-node two-sided risk. Our underweight is a policy call: the evidence read points to tightness, not oversupply, so a policy truce would make it wrong.
Sources: NVIDIA Q3 FY27; TrendForce 18 Nov 2026; BIS final rule 5 Nov 2026; MarketXLS 1 Nov 202612 / 14

13Bottom line: the industry is bigger than the trade

Our judgment

  • 2026 was a memory-price event, not a broad unit boom: memory 54% of revenue vs 27% in 2025.
  • The AI build-out is real but financed — the risk has migrated from demand to balance sheets.
  • At 13x forward the sector is cheap versus the S&P 500 (17.8x) and versus its own end-2025 level (26.1x); that is an opportunity and a warning.
  • We would be overweight foundry, WFE and analog, neutral accelerators, own HBM scarcity while sizing commodity memory small, underweight China-exposed mature node.

What we are not saying

  • No single-stock recommendations or price targets.
  • No claim that the cycle has peaked — the data as of 20 Nov 2026 shows no order cuts.
  • No claim that 13x is a floor: the SOX fell 46.5% in 2022.
