# HKUST Opens Homegrown AI Chatbot to Every Student and Staff Member — and Leaves the Coursework Rules to Each Instructor

*A news report for HKUST students and faculty*

*Lead: HKUST announced on 22 April 2025 that all staff and students can use HKGAI V1, Hong Kong's homegrown large language model, at no cost — the first local university to do so, according to the university. The rollout is the clearest sign yet of how HKUST wants AI used on campus, and it lands on a teaching policy that leaves the rules for each course in your instructor's hands.*

---

## What happened

On **22 April 2025**, HKUST announced that all staff and students can use **HKGAI V1**, Hong Kong's first homegrown large language model, **at no cost**. The model was developed by the Hong Kong Generative AI Research and Development Center (HKGAI) and had previously been available only to civil servants in a pilot. HKUST says it is the first local university to open it to its whole campus community, via the university's one-stop AI platform.

It sits on three earlier building blocks — the third of which is the teaching policy that decides how you may use AI in your coursework:

- **A university-run GenAI platform.** HKUST runs its own ChatGPT-style platform on secure cloud infrastructure, with chat data protected and visible only to the user, and monthly prompt limits subsidised by the university. Faculty and students are encouraged to use GenAI in teaching and learning.
- **Course-level rules, in writing.** Since **Fall 2023-24**, HKUST has let each instructor set their own course-level GenAI policy — and requires them to communicate that choice to students **in writing**.
- **AI in the curriculum.** HKUST announced **five new undergraduate programmes for 2025/26** (28–29 October 2024), two AI-focused: a **BEng in Artificial Intelligence** (School of Engineering) and **Data Analytics and Artificial Intelligence in Science** (School of Science). It also offers an **Extended Major Program in Artificial Intelligence** as an add-on to existing majors.

## Why it matters to you

**1. The teaching and assessment rules are the part that affects your grades.** Because policy is set course by course, two courses in the same term can have opposite rules on AI, and the same submission can be fine in one and misconduct in another. Read the GenAI statement in every course outline before you submit; if it is missing, ask the instructor in writing.

**2. "Free and private" is a real difference from the public tools.** The platform's selling point is that chat data stay with the user and are not fed into a public model. For students handling sensitive coursework or research data, that matters: HKUST's published research guidance (as summarised by the University AI Policy Tracker) says sensitive or restricted data should not be entered into external AI systems without authorisation.
**3. Usage is already mainstream.** A peer-reviewed survey of HKUST staff and students in the *Journal of Asian Public Policy* reports that **44.71% of student respondents used ChatGPT five times or more**, and **91.69% said they intended to keep using it as a learning tool**. (These figures come from the article's abstract; the full text was not accessible to us, so treat them as indicative rather than definitive.)

**4. The integrity line has not moved.** Course syllabi in HKUST's Division of Public Policy state that using AI to fabricate references "constitutes plagiarism and is thus unacceptable", and that "academic misconduct need not be intentional… ultimately, it is you and not AI that will be held accountable". Sanctions under the academic-integrity regulations are graduated and can include a notation of academic misconduct on your transcript.

**5. Detection is not the strategy — assessment design is.** HKUST's Centre for Education Innovation (CEI) publishes guidance on assessment design, prompt engineering and responsible use; a third-party policy tracker records that CEI advises designing assessments that account for GenAI rather than relying solely on AI-text detectors. For faculty, that means the assessment brief, not a detector, is where the rule is enforced.

**6. How HKUST compares.** HKU temporarily banned ChatGPT in early 2023 and has since folded AI into the curriculum as a "fifth literacy"; CUHK's stated default is that AI use is prohibited unless explicitly permitted; PolyU publishes teacher guidance on GenAI in assessment. (These comparisons rest on secondary sources and should be read as a sketch, not a ranking.) HKUST's model — instructor-set rules plus a university-provided tool — puts the weight on students and instructors reading the rules together.

## The teaching and assessment angle, in short

HKUST's approach is not a campus-wide ban or a campus-wide permission: it is a written, course-level policy each instructor sets and must state in writing, backed by a university-provided tool and CEI guidance on assessment design.

We flag these rather than fill the gaps.

## What to do next

- **Students:** check the GenAI statement in every course outline this term; use the university platform for anything sensitive; cite and disclose AI help where your course requires it.
- **Faculty:** put your course-level GenAI policy in writing in the outline, and state plainly what counts as permitted help versus misconduct.
- **Everyone:** HKGAI V1 access is live now via the university's AI platform.
