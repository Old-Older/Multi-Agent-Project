What we are being asked to decide

The question

After a year in which the chip index rose ~86% to June, then fell ~38%, is 2027 a year to add semiconductor risk, hold it, or take it down?

Our answer in one line

Own the bottleneck, not the beta. The demand is contracted and the supply is physically constrained; the risk has migrated from valuation to financing and memory pricing. We recommend a benchmark-weight, quality-tilted position with explicit memory and AI-capex hedges.

Accelerators: a boom with a share shift inside it

Merchant (NVIDIA)

  • Q3 FY27 revenue $115.8bn (+103% YoY); data center $107.6bn (+110%)
  • Annualised data-center run-rate ≈ $430bn
  • Vera Rubin: ~$20bn in its first quarter — fastest ramp in company history
  • Forward P/E ~14x vs 1-yr mean 22.5x

Custom ASIC (Broadcom, Google TPU)

  • Broadcom AI semis $16.7bn in Q3 FY26, +221% YoY; FY26 $56–58bn
  • TPU compute shipments doubled QoQ to ~2m H100-equivalents (Epoch AI estimate)
  • AMD data center $8.55bn (+97%), Q4 guide $17bn total

Why this matters for the portfolio

Custom silicon is growing roughly twice as fast as merchant off a ~$60bn base. That is share shift inside a boom, not a demand problem — but it changes who captures the margin.

It also concentrates customer risk: Broadcom's next leg depends on Anthropic (largest XPU customer next year) and OpenAI (second by FY2028).

Implication: own the accelerator complex through the foundry and packaging layer, which is paid by merchant and custom alike, rather than betting on which architecture wins.

Sources: NVIDIA Q3 FY27 release (17 Nov 2026); AMD Q3-26 (3 Nov 2026); Broadcom via KuCoin/Barchart (Sep–Oct 2026); Epoch AI (3 Nov 2026).

Memory: the 2026 market was a price event, not a unit event

  • WSTS: 2026 market $1.51tn (+90%), of which memory ≈ +250% to >$800bn — memory is over half the market vs ~30% historically
  • H1-2026 actual $702bn (+102%); 2027 forecast ~$1.9tn (+27%)
  • 4Q26 contract prices: DRAM +10–15% QoQ, NAND +15–20% QoQ; market "undersupplied"
  • HBM ASP forecast to more than double in 2027 (TrendForce, 18 Nov 2026); HBM4 shipping at all three suppliers for Vera Rubin
  • HBM share Q2-26: SK hynix 50%, Samsung 33%, Micron third

The mechanism

HBM consumes ≈22% of top-3 DRAM wafer starts for ~9% of bits. The shortage is a wafer-allocation shortage, which is why conventional DRAM is squeezed and prices compound.

Supply relief is 2027–28 at the earliest: SK hynix pulled Yongin completion forward to 2034–35; Samsung + SK hynix plan $518bn of multi-year Korean memory capacity.

But: TrendForce and GF Securities both see NAND in surplus in 2027. Memory is the most cyclical part of this thesis.

Sources: WSTS (2 Jun 2026, Aug 2026); TrendForce (30 Sep 2026, 18 Nov 2026); Counterpoint via Businesskorea (2 Sep 2026); Forbes (12 Jul 2026). HBM market size (~$58bn) is single-sourced — see appendix.

Supply: the constraint moved from wafers to packaging

  • TSMC 3Q26 revenue NT$1,494bn (+41% YoY in US$); FY26 growth guided ~44%; "demand remained above available supply"
  • Capex $60–64bn/yr for 2026–28; broker 2028 estimates up to $105bn
  • CoWoS booking windows 52–78 weeks; 100% of chips still packaged in Taiwan even when front-end runs in Arizona
  • Pricing power: +3–6% wafer hikes from 2027 (steepest on N2/N3); 5–20% "location premium" on US-made wafers
  • Arizona commitment raised to $265bn; overseas fabs dilute gross margin ~3–4pp

Second sources are arriving — slowly

  • Samsung Taylor 2nm fully booked before mass production (Tesla, Broadcom, Arm); Tesla AI5 in trial production
  • Intel: external foundry revenue only $174m in Q1-26, but a first outside 18A customer signed in Nov-26 at ~70% yield
  • Equipment: SEMI 2026 sales revised up to $167bn (+23%), 2027 $156bn

Implication: pricing power sits with the bottleneck — foundry, advanced packaging, HBM and the equipment that enables them. That is where we want the exposure.

Valuation: the de-rating was earnings-led, not price-led

14,655SOX peak, 22 Jun 2026
−12.2%single day, 5 Nov 2026 — worst since Mar 2020
13.0xSOX forward P/E — lowest since Jan 2019
  • Rolling 12-month EPS estimates rose +155% from end-2025 while the multiple compressed to ~13x. The market stopped paying for AI growth; it did not deny it.
  • For the first time in the AI boom the SOX trades at a discount to the S&P 500 (13.7x vs 18.3x NTM, BofA/FactSet; Morningstar puts the SOX at 13.0x — two providers, same direction).
  • Concentration risk sits in the benchmark, not the chip index: top-10 = ~38–43% of the S&P 500, NVDA alone ~7.7–9.4% of the capped SOX.

Judgment: valuation is no longer the risk — the forecast is. At 13x, the market is already discounting a 2027 disappointment. That is an asymmetric setup, but only if the earnings estimate holds.

Investment implications: what we would own and why

Overweight

  • Foundry & advanced packaging — paid by merchant and custom alike; pricing power (+3–6% 2027 hikes, US location premium)
  • HBM / memory with HBM mix — ASP to more than double in 2027; supply relief only 2027–28
  • Semicap — 2026 equipment sales revised up to $167bn (+23%); note SEMI's 2027 forecast falls to $156bn, so this is a 2026–early-2027 trade, not a multi-year one
  • Custom-ASIC enablers — growing ~2x faster than merchant

Neutral / underweight

  • Commodity NAND & DRAM — 2027 surplus risk (TrendForce, GF Securities)
  • China-exposed revenue — underwrite zero; NVIDIA China share already 34%
  • Single-customer AI names — Anthropic/OpenAI concentration at Broadcom
  • Levered AI-infrastructure plays — neoclouds, GPU-rental models

Portfolio construction

Benchmark weight overall, quality tilt within. Size the position so a repeat of the −38.5% drawdown is survivable — i.e. a position whose loss at −40% stays inside the fund's risk budget (to be set with risk). Use the capped SOX rather than the concentrated S&P 500 as the expression of the theme. The AI-capex hedge is explicit: no levered AI-infrastructure or neocloud exposure, and a cap on any single customer-concentrated name.

This is an analytical view for internal discussion, not a trade instruction. Position sizing must be set against the fund's mandate and risk budget.

What we would do next

Decisions requested today

  • Approve a benchmark-weight, quality-tilted semiconductor position with an explicit HBM/foundry overweight and commodity-memory underweight
  • Approve a risk budget sized to a −40% drawdown scenario
  • Approve quarterly review against the five triggers on the previous slide

Open questions for the team

  • Do we express the theme through the capped SOX or through single names? (Concentration differs materially.)
  • What is our maximum China revenue exposure tolerance?
  • Do we hedge the memory leg separately, given the 2027 NAND surplus call?

Bottom line: the industry's 2027 problem is not demand. It is whether the buyers can keep financing it — and whether memory prices hold. We want to be paid for the bottleneck, not for the beta.
