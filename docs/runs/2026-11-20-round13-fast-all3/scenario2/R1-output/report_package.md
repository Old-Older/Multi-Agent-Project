## 1. MAIN WEB ARTICLE

### Headline
Six credits of AI, no exceptions: HKUST makes AI literacy a graduation requirement

### Standfirst
From 2026-27, every new HKUST undergraduate must complete six credits of AI and data literacy under a new Common Core area, HAIC. The first course, AISC 1000, is a three-credit, first-year, self-paced module. Here is what changes — and what the university has not yet said.

### Body

HKUST will require every undergraduate admitted from the 2026-27 academic year onward to complete six dedicated credits of artificial intelligence and data literacy, under a new Common Core area called Human-AI Co-Creation and Data Literacy (HAIC). The university announced the mandatory AI Common Core on 13 July 2026, alongside new comprehensive scholarships aimed at attracting top talent.

The Office of the Provost frames the change in blunt terms on its HAIC Horizon page: "Every HKUST graduate, ready for the age of AI." The same page states that all undergraduates admitted from 2026-27 onward "will complete six dedicated credits developing the AI and data literacy they need to learn, work, and lead" in a world shaped by AI.

The first course in the sequence is already in the 2026-27 catalogue. AISC 1000, "Foundations of AI Literacy: Human-AI Orchestration", is a three-credit course for first-year undergraduates, delivered in a self-paced, blended format. The catalogue describes it as equipping students with the cognitive, technical and ethical foundations of working with AI. The Common Core Program's curriculum page confirms that HAIC is a new area for students admitted in 2026-27, distinct from the Common Core taken by the cohorts admitted between 2022-23 and 2025-26. Degree requirement sheets for 2026-27, including the BEng in Artificial Intelligence, already carry the requirement.

**Who is covered — and who is not**

The requirement applies to students entering from 2026-27. Students already enrolled under the previous Common Core are not covered by the six-credit rule, according to the Common Core Office's own comparison of cohorts. In practice, that means two groups of undergraduates will be on campus at the same time with different graduation requirements.

HKUST has not said publicly whether the six-credit requirement extends to undergraduates at its Guangzhou campus, HKUST(GZ). The public documents reviewed for this report describe the requirement in terms of HKUST undergraduates admitted from 2026-27 and do not address HKUST(GZ) coverage either way. Students at the Guangzhou campus should not assume the rule applies to them until the university confirms it.

Nor do the public documents reviewed specify how the six credits are to be assembled — for example, whether students take two three-credit HAIC courses or one core course plus approved electives. That detail is not publicly confirmed.

**Why it matters**

Most universities that have moved on generative AI have done so through guidance: what students may use, how they should cite it, and where instructors draw the line. HKUST has taken a different route. By placing AI and data literacy inside the Common Core — the part of the curriculum every undergraduate must complete — the university has made it a condition of graduation rather than an optional skill.

That shift matters for two reasons. First, it settles the question of whether AI competence is core business for a university: at HKUST, from this intake, it is. Second, it puts the burden of delivery on departments and faculty, who must now design, staff and timetable HAIC courses for every incoming student, and decide how a self-paced, blended first-year course is assessed.

**A different approach from guidance-only policies**

It is worth being precise about what has changed. HKUST has not banned generative AI, and it has not left the question to individual instructors alone. It has done something more structural: it has written AI literacy into the part of the curriculum that no undergraduate can avoid. A student who never takes a computing course, never joins an AI lab and never writes a line of code will still leave HKUST with six credits of formal AI and data literacy on their transcript.

That has consequences beyond the classroom. Employers and graduate schools reading a HKUST transcript from 2027 onward will be able to assume a baseline of AI competence that earlier cohorts were never required to demonstrate. Within the university, the requirement also creates a shared vocabulary: students from engineering, business, science and the humanities will all have studied the same foundational questions about how AI systems are built, what data they depend on and where the ethical limits lie.

The context is not a blank slate. HKUST already runs its own GenAI platform for staff and students, which a peer-reviewed survey study of the university describes as running on secure cloud infrastructure with ChatGPT-like functionality, chat data accessible only to the user, and monthly prompt limits subsidised by the university. The Center for Education Innovation publishes guidance on generative AI in higher education, including a guidebook dated July 2026. The HAIC requirement builds on that "encourage and support" stance rather than replacing it.

**What is still open**

The university has not published a detailed account of how HAIC courses will be staffed or assessed, and no official statement from the Provost or the Center for Education Innovation on those points was available for this report. Nor is it clear how a self-paced, blended first-year course will be examined, or what support will be offered to students who arrive with little prior exposure to data or programming.

Those are the questions to watch as the 2026-27 year begins. For students, the practical advice is simple: read your own programme's requirement sheet rather than relying on what a friend in another cohort was told. For faculty, the work starts now — the requirement is fixed, but the courses that will satisfy it are still being built.

### What it means for you — students

- **If you enter in 2026-27 or later:** six credits of AI and data literacy are compulsory. Plan them into your first year, not your final one.
- **AISC 1000 is the entry point:** three credits, first-year, self-paced and blended. Check the course catalogue for the current description before you enrol.
- **If you are already enrolled:** the six-credit HAIC requirement does not apply to you; you remain on the Common Core for your cohort. Check your own programme's requirement sheet if you are unsure.
- **HKUST(GZ) students:** the public documents reviewed do not state whether the requirement covers you. Ask your programme office before planning courses around it.
- **The structure is not yet public:** how the six credits break down into courses has not been confirmed in the documents reviewed.

### A note for faculty

HAIC is a curriculum obligation, not just a policy statement. Every undergraduate admitted from 2026-27 must complete six credits in the new area, which means courses must exist, be staffed and be assessed — including AISC 1000, a first-year, self-paced, blended course. The public record does not yet set out staffing models, assessment design or workload arrangements for HAIC teaching. Departments planning 2026-27 offerings should confirm details with the Common Core Office and the Center for Education Innovation, whose generative-AI guidance (guidebook dated July 2026) remains the reference point for classroom practice.
