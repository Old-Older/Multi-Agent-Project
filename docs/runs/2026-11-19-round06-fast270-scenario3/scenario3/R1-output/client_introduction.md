# Your US Stock Plan — A Short Introduction

**For:** a 25-year-old Hong Kong investor · **Amount:** HKD 1,000,000 · **Date:** 18 November 2026
**Please note:** this is a simulated, illustrative plan — not a solicitation and not personal advice. No orders have been placed. Every number below comes from the full proposal document, and all of them are **ranges, not forecasts**.

---

## 1. What the plan is

You told us you can leave this money alone for 10+ years, you want long-term growth, and you could sit through a 30% fall without selling. That is a strong starting position — at 25, time and patience are your two biggest advantages.

So the plan is deliberately simple: **own a big, cheap slice of the US stock market, add a modest tilt towards technology, keep a little outside the US, and hold some cash** so you never have to sell at a bad moment.

| What you own | How much | In dollars |
|---|---|---|
| VOO — the 500 largest US companies | 45% | HKD 450,000 |
| VTI — the whole US market (adds mid- and small-caps) | 20% | HKD 200,000 |
| QQQM — the 100 largest Nasdaq (tech-heavy) companies | 15% | HKD 150,000 |
| IEFA — developed markets outside the US | 10% | HKD 100,000 |
| Cash / USD money-market fund | 10% | HKD 100,000 |

In total, about **80% of your money is in US shares**.

## 2. Why it is split that way

- **Core first (65%).** VOO plus VTI gives you the broad US market for a fee of 0.03% — roughly **HKD 300 a year** on HKD 650,000. Cheap and wide beats clever and narrow.
- **A measured tech tilt (15%), not a bet.** QQQM leans towards the biggest tech and platform companies. It has historically added both extra return and extra ups and downs, in roughly equal measure. 15% is enough to matter, small enough to survive a tech selloff.
- **Some non-US ballast (10%).** The US is about 60–65% of the world's stock market. Owning only the US is a single-country bet; IEFA reduces that a little — it is a tilt, not a full hedge. In fact, because the US is 60–65% of the world, this plan is still an **active overweight to the US**.
- **10% cash is a feature, not laziness.** It lets you buy in gradually (see below) and means an emergency never forces you to sell at the wrong time.
- **No borrowing, no single-stock picking.** At your age you don't need leverage to grow your money — borrowing mainly turns a temporary fall into a permanent loss.

**Be honest about the overlap:** VOO, VTI and QQQM all hold the same giant tech names, so your true S&P 500 weight is about **62%**, not 45%. The extra mid- and small-cap exposure VTI adds is only about **4%** of the portfolio.

**How you get in:** you don't invest it all on day one. You put in **HKD 360,000 now**, **HKD 270,000 in month 3** and **HKD 270,000 in month 6**. This is a comfort tool, not a return booster — it may cost you a little if markets rise meanwhile. If you miss a date, invest at the next one; don't wait for a "better" price. After that, review once a year and rebalance.

## 3. What it will cost

- **Fund fees:** about **HKD 300–1,000 a year** in total.
- **Tax on dividends:** the US takes **30%** of dividends paid to Hong Kong investors on US-listed funds. On a typical 1.5% dividend yield that is about **0.45% a year, roughly HKD 4,500**. Irish-domiciled versions of the same funds (e.g. CSPX, VUAA) would roughly halve this — worth discussing.
- **Currency conversion:** about **HKD 3,000** once, at a 0.3% spread. Convert once if you can, rather than three times.
- **Good news:** Hong Kong charges **no capital gains tax** and **no tax on foreign dividends** for individuals.

## 4. What could go wrong

This is the honest part, and it matters more than the rest.

- **The portfolio will fall sometimes.** A 30–50% drop is normal roughly once a decade. On HKD 1,000,000 that is **−HKD 300,000 to −HKD 500,000** at the worst point. Historically it has recovered within a few years — but only if you don't sell.
- **Your funds overlap.** VOO, VTI and QQQM all hold the same giant tech names, so a tech-led selloff hits about 80% of your money at once.
- **US shares are not cheap today.** The S&P 500 trades at about 18 times expected earnings. Returns over the next decade may be lower than the past decade's.
- **Rising interest rates hurt the tech tilt most.** The QQQM part of the portfolio is the most sensitive to rate rises.
- **Currency.** The HKD is pegged between 7.75 and 7.85 per USD (today 7.8458), so currency moves are small — at most about ±1.3% — but real.
- **US estate tax.** US-listed holdings above **USD 60,000** can face up to **40% US estate tax** for non-residents. About **90% of this portfolio (≈USD 114,700)** is US-situs, so potential exposure is up to about **USD 45,000**. This is a real planning issue: ask about Irish-domiciled funds (CSPX/VUAA) and get cross-border tax advice before investing more.
- **Your broker matters.** Use a regulated (ideally SFC-licensed) broker. SIPC protection covers up to USD 500,000, but only for broker failure — not market losses.
- **The biggest risk is you.** Selling in a panic destroys more long-term returns than any fee. Decide now, in writing, that you will not sell during a drawdown.

**What you might reasonably expect** (illustrative, not a promise): a long-run return of **5–8% a year in USD**, nominal, after fees and before tax (about **4.5–7.5%** after tax drag); a typical year swinging **15–18%**, with roughly one year in six worse than about **−10% to −13%**; a worst realistic year of **−35% to −45%**. At 6% a year, HKD 1,000,000 would grow to about **HKD 1.79m in 10 years** and **HKD 3.21m in 20 years** (before tax). Those are planning figures, not forecasts.

## 5. What to do next

1. **Read the full proposal** and check the split and the risks feel right to you.
2. **Confirm the tax points** — the 30%/15% dividend rates and the US estate-tax exposure — with a tax adviser and the fund documents. These were not independently re-verified.
3. **Choose a regulated broker** and compare its FX spread and fees.
4. **Write your "do not sell in a drawdown" rule** and put it somewhere you'll see it.
5. **Place Tranche 1 (HKD 360,000)** only once you're comfortable — and tell us if anything here doesn't fit your situation.

*Questions? Ask. It is much better to ask now than to sell in a panic later.*
