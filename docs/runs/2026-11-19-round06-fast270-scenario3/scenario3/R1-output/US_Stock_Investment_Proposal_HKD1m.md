**Read the overlap honestly.** VOO + VTI + QQQM = **80% in one market**, and VTI already contains the S&P 500. True S&P 500 weight is ≈ **62%** (VOO 45% + VTI's large-cap share ≈17%); the mid/small-cap add is only ≈4% of the portfolio. Mega-cap tech names appear in all three funds. The 10% IEFA is **an active US overweight** (the US is ~60–65% of global market cap), not true global diversification — it is a deliberate tilt for a growth brief, not a hedge.

### Why this mix
1. **Core first.** 65% in VOO + VTI gives you the broad US market at 0.03% cost — about **HKD 300/year** on HKD 650,000. Over 10 years, a 0.15% fee difference compounds to roughly 1.5% of terminal value; a 1% fee difference to ~10%.
2. **A measured growth tilt, not a bet.** 15% QQQM concentrates in the largest tech/platform companies. It has historically added return and volatility in roughly equal measure. 15% is enough to matter, small enough to survive a tech-led drawdown.
3. **Some non-US ballast.** 10% IEFA reduces single-country concentration, though it is a tilt, not a hedge.
4. **10% cash is a feature, not laziness.** It funds the entry plan in §4 and means you never have to sell at a bad moment for an emergency.
5. **No leverage, no single stocks.** At 25 with a 10-year horizon you do not need leverage to compound; leverage mainly converts a temporary drawdown into a permanent loss.

## 4. Entry plan (phased, to manage timing risk)
- **Tranche 1 (now):** 40% of the equity allocation (HKD 360,000).
- **Tranche 2 (month 3):** 30% (HKD 270,000).
- **Tranche 3 (month 6):** 30% (HKD 270,000).
- **Missed-tranche rule:** if you miss a scheduled tranche, invest it at the next scheduled date — do not wait for a "better" price. If markets fall more than 15% before a tranche is due, bring that tranche forward.
- **Phasing is a behaviour tool, not a return-enhancer.** It has a small expected cost (you may under-invest if markets rise during the six months); its value is that it makes starting easier and reduces regret.
- **FX cost:** converting HKD→USD costs roughly 0.1–0.5% spread; at 0.3% that is ≈ **HKD 3,000** on HKD 1,000,000 — comparable to several years of fund fees. Three tranches = three conversions unless you use a multi-currency account and convert once.
- Keep the 10% cash buffer; top it up from savings if you want a larger reserve.
- **Rebalance** annually, or when any holding drifts more than 5 percentage points from target.

## 5. Key risks — and what they mean in dollars
*Dollar figures below are historical/statistical ranges, not predictions; actual outcomes may be worse.*

| Risk | What it looks like | Illustrative impact on HKD 1,000,000 |
|---|---|---|
| **Market / drawdown risk** | A 30–50% bear market is normal once a decade | **On total assets:** −HKD 300,000 to −500,000 at the trough. **On the 90% equity sleeve:** a 33–56% equity decline is needed for the same dollar loss (cash does not fall) |
| **Concentration / tech risk** | VOO, VTI and QQQM overlap heavily in the same mega-cap tech names | A tech-led selloff hits ~80% of the portfolio at once |
| **Valuation risk** | Forward P/E ~18x leaves little room for earnings disappointment | Returns could be below historical averages for years |
| **Interest-rate / duration risk** | Long-duration growth/tech earnings are most sensitive to rate rises (10-yr Treasury yield not verified — §8) | The QQQM tilt is the most rate-sensitive part of the portfolio |
| **Currency risk** | USD/HKD moves within 7.75–7.85 | Max ≈ ±1.3% on the HKD value; small but real |
| **Tax drag** | 30% US withholding on dividends (US-domiciled funds) | On a 1.5% yield ≈ 0.45%/yr ≈ HKD 4,500/yr; Irish UCITS would roughly halve this |
| **US estate tax** | US-situs assets above a **USD 60,000 per-person** exemption (not indexed) can face up to 40% US estate tax for non-residents | This portfolio is ≈**90% US-situs (≈USD 114,700)**; potential exposure up to ≈**USD 45,000** before any credit. **This is a design issue, not a footnote — see below** |
| **Platform / custody risk** | Broker failure, SFC-licensed vs offshore broker, SIPC limits | SIPC covers up to USD 500,000 but **only for broker failure, not market losses**; US-listed ETF holdings are US-situs for estate tax |
| **Geopolitical / regulatory risk** | Tariffs, sanctions, or US policy affecting foreign holders | Hard to quantify; a reason to keep some assets outside the US |
| **Behaviour risk** | Selling in a panic | The single largest destroyer of long-term returns |

**Estate-tax design choice (primary, not optional):** if the estate-tax exposure matters to you, hold the core in **Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs (e.g. CSPX for S&P 500, VUAA for S&P 500 acc.)** through a HK broker instead of VOO/VTI. That removes US-situs exposure and roughly halves dividend withholding. The trade-off is slightly higher fees and wider spreads. **Confirm with a cross-border tax adviser before investing more than USD 60,000 in US-domiciled funds.**

**Other mitigants:** phased entry, 10% cash, annual rebalancing, a written "do not sell in a drawdown" rule, and a regulated (ideally SFC-licensed) broker.

## 6. What to expect (illustrative, not a forecast)
- **Expected long-run return:** a reasonable planning range is **5–8% p.a. in USD, nominal, after fund fees but before tax**. After estimated tax drag (≈0.45%/yr) that is roughly **4.5–7.5% p.a.**
- **Compounding at 6% p.a. (nominal, USD, gross of tax):** HKD 1,000,000 → ≈ **HKD 1.79m in 10 years**, ≈ **HKD 3.21m in 20 years**. After tax drag the figures would be lower.
- **Expected volatility:** roughly **15–18% p.a.** In a normal year, roughly **one year in six would be worse than about −10% to −13%** (mean 6% minus one standard deviation). This is a frequency statement, not a floor.
- **Worst realistic year:** −35% to −45%. **Best realistic year:** +30% to +40%. These are historical/statistical ranges, not predictions.
- **Time to recover a 30% drawdown:** historically 1–3 years.
