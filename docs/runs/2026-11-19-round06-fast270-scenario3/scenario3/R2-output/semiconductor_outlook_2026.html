Executive summary

Five judgments

  • 1. 2026 was a memory price event, not a demand event. Industry revenue +89.9% to USD 1.51tn, but memory alone went from USD 230bn to USD 804bn (+249.5%) and is ~53% of industry revenue vs ~28% in 2025. Underwrite 2027–30 on normalised memory, not on 2026 run-rates. WSTS
  • 2. Near-term visibility is unusually high; the risk is 2028. CoWoS (~200k wpm) and HBM are booked through 2027, with TSMC CoWoS capacity expanding >60% — the shortage eases exactly as 2026–27 capacity lands. TrendForce
  • 3. The first digestion signal has appeared. Microsoft and Google both reported Q3-2026 capex below consensus (29 Oct 2026). One quarter is not a trend — Q4 prints and 2027 guides are the test. CNBC
  • 4. Own the enablers, not the winner. Foundry, advanced packaging, HBM, equipment and EDA/IP monetise merchant GPUs and custom ASICs; the merchant-GPU single name carries the concentration risk.
  • 5. Position size is the real risk decision. A plausible correlated stress is −35% to −50% on a concentrated sleeve; that argues for a capped sleeve with mechanical rebalancing, not for avoiding the sector.

Demand

AI capex: still rising, but the slope is the question

What the forecasts say

Hyperscaler capex (MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN/META): USD 785bn in 2026, approaching USD 1tn in 2027 (Moody's, May 2026). Morgan Stanley models combined data-centre capex from USD 466bn (2025) to a ~USD 1.47tn peak in 2027, then +12% in 2028. secondary

What the data says

On 29 Oct 2026 both Microsoft and Google reported Q3 capex below analyst estimates; Microsoft cited reliance on third-party providers. This is the earliest hard datapoint of capex discipline. CNBC headline

Concentration

Four AI chip designers absorb ~90% of global CoWoS and HBM supply; NVIDIA holds ~50%+ of TSMC advanced CoWoS through 2027. TrendForce

Our read

2027 is largely contracted. The investable question is 2028: whether capex growth decelerates to low-teens (MS base case) or rolls over. We size for the second without forecasting it.

Forecast dispersion is wide and internally inconsistent (one MS note has 2027 capex +57% y/y, another a ~USD 1.47tn peak). Treat 2027 as a range, not a point estimate.

Supply

Sold out through 2027 — and that is the 2028 problem

  • Advanced packaging is the binding constraint. Industry CoWoS capacity ~200k wafers/month in 2026; TSMC capacity booked through 2027; TSMC's chairman said on the 16 Jul 2026 call that packaging capacity now limits customer growth. TrendForce / call
  • The relief arrives in 2027–28. TSMC CoWoS output expanding >60%, with the shortage expected to ease from 2027; 14-reticle CoWoS targeted for 2028 and panel-level CoPoS for late 2028–29. TrendForce
  • Memory supply is the swing factor. Memory is ~53% of industry revenue, so memory-maker capex decisions in 2027 set 2028 supply. We could not verify SK Hynix / Samsung / Micron 2027 capex plans — this is the single most important missing input for the 2028 call. unverified
  • Implication: earnings visibility is high for 2027 and low for 2028. Pay for 2027 visibility, but do not capitalise 2028 at 2026 margins.

China & geopolitics

A bifurcated control regime, and a mature-node cycle already turning

Policy: looser on chips, tighter on tools

13 Jan 2026 BIS final rule revised licence review for advanced AI chips to China (H200-class, case-by-case) BIS headline. 22 Jun 2026: controls expanded to 12 equipment categories for HBM stacking, hybrid bonding and 3D IC testing single source.

Mature nodes: oversupply is in the price

China added 1.2–1.5m wafer starts/month at 28–55nm, running at ~60% utilisation, with MCU prices down 20–30% low-quality source. China is ~22% of global 200mm capacity (SEMI) and roughly half of all mature-node capacity (CSIS) — definitions differ, do not add them.

Taiwan concentration

TSMC = 72.3% of global foundry revenue (Q1-2026, TrendForce) and >90% of ≤3nm logic secondary. Leading-edge logic and advanced packaging sit in one earthquake- and blockade-exposed geography.

Investment read

Mature-node price pressure is an earnings risk today for analog/MCU/power names, not a 2027 story. Prefer names with <15% China revenue or a genuine installed-base/service business.

Decision & next steps

What we are asking the team to approve

  • Approve the framework: a capped semiconductor sleeve (10–15% of a medium-risk portfolio) built on the six-bucket structure, with the stated caps and mechanical rebalancing.
  • Approve the 2027 stance: own the enablers (foundry, packaging, HBM, equipment, EDA/IP) rather than a single merchant-GPU bet; keep 5–10% dry powder.
  • Set the 2028 trigger: if two consecutive quarters of declining aggregate hyperscaler capex guidance appear, cut the accelerator sleeve to the low end and raise cash — do not wait for memory prices to confirm.
  • Close the data gaps before client use: WSTS Autumn 2026 forecast; Q4-2026 DRAM/NAND/HBM contract prices; consensus forward P/E and 2026/27 EPS growth for the eight names; memory-maker 2027 capex; SOX level and YTD return.
  • What would change our mind: a sustained capex re-acceleration with power constraints easing would justify the top of every range; a Taiwan quarantine or a memory price roll-over would justify the bottom.
