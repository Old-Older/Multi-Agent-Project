## 2. Key macro drivers

**Fed policy — the regime has turned hawkish again.**
- Target range **3.75%–4.00%**, after a **+25bp hike effective 17 Sep 2026** (first hike of the cycle); the Fed then held at the 28 Oct 2026 meeting (10–2 vote). (FRED via Ironclad Research, 16 Nov 2026; FOMC statement, 28 Oct 2026)
- Governor Waller (10 Nov 2026): if core CPI keeps running ~0.3% m/m, "a 50 basis point hike … should be considered at the December meeting." KPMG expects another hike in December (KPMG, 10 Nov 2026).
- **Implication:** the "Fed cutting cycle" narrative of 2024–25 is over for now. Rate risk is two-sided and skewed to hikes — a headwind for long-duration growth stocks and for bond prices.

**Inflation — re-accelerating.**
- October 2026 CPI **+0.4% m/m** (Sept +0.6%); core +0.3% m/m. BLS did not publish a y/y rate because of missing 2025 data from the government shutdown; KPMG's interpolation implies **~4.2% y/y**; inflationdata.com estimates ~4.0%. (BLS, 10 Nov 2026; KPMG, 10 Nov 2026)
- Core PCE held at **3.1%** in September 2026; Q3 GDP grew 3.4%. (rckanalytics, 30 Oct 2026)
- **Implication:** inflation is above target and sticky — the main reason the Fed is hiking, and the main risk to both equity multiples and bond returns.

**Rates and the USD/HKD peg.**
- 10-year Treasury yield **5.12%** (16 Nov 2026), up ~1.0pp y/y; 2-year 4.83%; 10y–2y spread +0.30pp. (FRED DGS10 via BasisPoint, 17 Nov 2026)
- The Linked Exchange Rate System (since 17 Oct 1983) holds USD/HKD in a **7.75–7.85 convertibility band**; HKMA sells HKD at 7.75 and buys at 7.85. Spot has been pinned near the **weak edge (~7.846)** with overnight HIBOR ~2.36% vs a 4.25% HKMA base rate. (HKMA; ECB reference via thetradersspread.com, 3 Nov 2026)
- **Implication for the client:** HKD is effectively a USD proxy, so **there is no material FX translation risk on USD assets** — but there is also **no FX diversification**. HK dollar rates track US rates, so HKD cash yields are low relative to USD cash (SGOV yields ~3.6% vs HKD deposits). The peg is a policy commitment, not a law of nature: a band change or break is a low-probability, high-impact tail risk (a former peg architect has publicly floated narrowing the band — Bloomberg, 29 Oct 2026).

**US–China / tariff risk.**
- The truce was **extended to 10 January 2027**: the US keeps the fentanyl-related tariff at 10% (cut from 20%) and suspends Section 301 maritime/logistics actions plus 178 exclusions; China keeps its 24% retaliatory tariff suspended but retains a 10% levy and continues pausing some export controls. (Supply Chain Dive / USTR & China MOF, 9 Nov 2026)
- **Implication:** tariff risk is *deferred, not resolved*. A hard deadline (10 Jan 2027) sits inside the investment horizon, and rare-earth/export-control compliance is still contested. Expect periodic volatility spikes around deadlines.

**Typical volatility (annualised, long-run, approximate — not from a single dated source):** S&P 500 ~15–16%; US total market ~16%; global all-cap ~15%; Nasdaq-100 ~20–22%; US small cap ~19–20%; US aggregate bonds ~5–6%; T-bills ~0.5%. *Flagged as approximate: I did not retrieve a dated volatility table, so treat these as order-of-magnitude.*

**Practical observations for a HKD 1m / USD 128k allocation:**
- **VOO vs VTI:** near-identical cost and behaviour; VTI adds ~10% mid/small-cap exposure and slightly less top-10 concentration. Either is a sound core; holding both is redundant.
- **VT** is the simplest one-fund answer and cuts single-country and single-stock risk (top-10 22.5% vs 37.3%), at the cost of ~40% non-US exposure the client did not ask for and a 0.03pp higher fee.
- **QQQ** is a leveraged bet on the same mega-cap tech already inside VOO — adding it increases concentration rather than diversifying. Its 0.18% fee is 6x VOO's.
- **BND** is not a reliable ballast when the Fed is hiking: it lost 0.7% over the past year despite a 4.2% yield. **SGOV** currently offers ~3.6% with essentially no price risk and is the cleaner "dry powder" vehicle for buying dips.
- **Currency:** all of these are USD-denominated; under the peg the HKD investor bears no material FX translation risk, but also gets no FX hedge benefit.

## 5. Bottom line for the proposal

1. **Valuation is reasonable on forward earnings but expensive on cyclically-adjusted earnings, and dangerously concentrated.** The bull case rests on 2026–27 EPS delivery (+24% / +13% consensus-style estimates); the bear case is a Fed hiking into 4%+ inflation with a 5.1% 10-year yield.
2. **The macro regime is the main near-term risk:** hikes (possibly 50bp in December), sticky ~4% CPI, a 5%+ 10-year, and a tariff deadline on 10 Jan 2027.
3. **History supports a long-horizon equity allocation but not a comfortable ride:** ~10.5% nominal p.a. long-run, but −37% to −49% drawdowns with 3–12 year recoveries are part of the deal.
4. **A sensible core** is VOO or VTI as the backbone, with a modest VT/VEA sleeve for diversification, optional small-cap (VB) for breadth, and SGOV (rather than BND) as the ballast/dip-buying reserve while the Fed is hiking. QQQ should be sized as a satellite, if at all.
