## 2. Market backdrop (as of the research date, November 2026)

- **Valuation is not cheap.** S&P 500 forward P/E ≈ **17.7x** (FactSet, 9 Nov 2026) vs a 5-year average of 19.8x and 10-year of 19.1x — i.e. *below* recent averages, but the Shiller CAPE at **~37.5–39.4x** is historically very high. Read: expected 10-year returns are likely **below** the historical ~10.5% p.a., and the market is priced for good news.
- **The index is concentrated.** The "Magnificent 7" are **~33–34%** of the S&P 500; the top 10 names are **~38%** of SPY. Buying "the market" today is largely a bet on a handful of mega-cap tech companies.
- **Rates are restrictive.** Fed funds **3.75–4.00%** after a September 2026 hike, with a possible further hike floated for December; 10-year Treasury at **5.12%**. Higher rates pressure valuations, especially long-duration growth stocks.
- **Inflation is sticky.** October CPI +0.4% m/m, roughly **4.0–4.2% y/y** (interpolated).
- **Currency.** USD/HKD is pinned near the **7.85 weak-side** of the 7.75–7.85 band under the Linked Exchange Rate System (spot ~7.846). Your HKD buys fewer dollars at the weak edge.
- **Long-run history still favours equities.** S&P 500 nominal CAGR ≈ **10.5% p.a. (1926–2024)**, ~7% real, positive in ~73% of years. Over **15- and 20-year rolling windows, returns have been positive 100% of the time** — but the worst 10-year window was slightly negative, and the worst single year was **−43.8% (1931)**.

**Bottom line:** the long-run case for owning US equities is intact; the *entry point* is mediocre and the *concentration* is high. That argues for owning the market broadly, phasing money in, and diversifying away from pure US mega-cap tech — not for staying in cash for a decade.

**Effective exposure:** ~70% US equity, ~15% non-US developed equity, ~15% defensive (bonds + T-bills). Blended equity beta ≈ 1.0; blended expense ratio ≈ **0.05%** (≈ HKD 500/year on HKD 1m, excluding brokerage commission, FX spread and any platform fee).

### Why this mix

1. **VOO + VTI (60%) is the core.** VTI already contains VOO, so together they give you the entire US market at a 0.03% fee with no stock-picking risk. This is the engine of long-term compounding. (Note the overlap: holding both is not extra diversification, it is a way to add mid- and small-cap exposure.)
2. **VEA (15%) is the deliberate diversifier.** US equities are ~62% of world market cap and are trading at a premium; developed ex-US trades at a much lower forward P/E (~14x vs ~18x). You are not abandoning the US thesis — you are refusing to bet 100% of your net worth on one country and one currency.
3. **QQQ (10%, capped).** You asked for maximum growth, and mega-cap tech has driven most of the last decade's returns. But QQQ's top 10 names are **46%** of the fund and it overlaps heavily with VOO. Capping it at 10% keeps the growth tilt without doubling your concentration. *The risk review recommended exactly this cap — the original draft had 15%.*
4. **BND + SGOV (15%).** Not there for returns — there for **behaviour and optionality**. They let you rebalance into a crash and give you a psychological anchor. Note BND returned **−0.7%** over the past year while the Fed hiked; SGOV (+3.8%) is the cleaner ballast right now. If rates peak and fall, BND becomes more valuable.
5. **No leverage, no individual stocks, no thematic/sector ETFs.** At 25 with no experience, single-stock and leveraged products are how first portfolios get destroyed.

## 4. Implementation plan (this matters as much as the allocation)

**Phase in over 6–12 months (dollar-cost averaging).** Do not invest HKD 1,000,000 on day one.

| Month | Action |
|---|---|
| Month 0 | Open brokerage account; complete **W-8BEN**; park the full HKD 1,000,000 in a HKD/USD money-market or SGOV-like vehicle |
| Months 1–6 | Invest **1/6 of the equity target each month** (~HKD 108,000/month into VOO/VTI/VEA/QQQ in target proportions) |
| Months 7–12 | Complete the remaining equity; build BND/SGOV to target |
| Ongoing | Rebalance when any holding drifts **±5 percentage points** from target, or once a year — whichever comes first |

**Why phase in?** With CAPE near 40x and the Fed still hiking, the entry point is uncertain. Phasing does not raise expected return much, but it dramatically reduces the chance that you start with a −20% loss in month one and quit. That behavioural benefit is worth more than the small expected-return cost.

**Automate it.** Set a standing monthly transfer and buy order. Decisions made in advance beat decisions made in a panic.

## 6. Risks — in plain terms and in HKD

| Risk | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Drawdown** | A −30% year = **−HKD 300,000** (portfolio 700,000). A −45% bear market = **−HKD 450,000** (550,000). A 2000/2008-style −55% = **−HKD 550,000** (450,000). You said you'd hold — this is the number you must genuinely be able to live with. |
| **Recovery math** | After −30% you need +43% to break even; after −45%, +82%; after −55%, +122%. Historically the S&P 500 took ~58 months to recover from the 2007–09 fall (−56.8%) and ~74 months from the 2000–03 fall (−49.1%). **A bad scenario could leave you underwater for 5–7 years — that is a possibility to plan for, not a forecast.** |
| **Valuation risk** | CAPE ~40x means future 10-year returns are likely below the 10.5% historical average. You may do everything right and still get 4–6% p.a. |
| **Concentration risk** | ~38% of the S&P 500 is 10 companies, mostly tech. A single sector de-rating (as in 2000–02, when the Nasdaq fell ~83%) hits you hard. VEA and the bond sleeve are your only real offsets. |
| **Currency risk** | USD/HKD is pegged 7.75–7.85, so FX risk is small *while the peg holds*. If the band ever changed, USD assets would be worth more in HKD (a gain for you) — but Hong Kong's imported inflation and rates would move too. Also: USD assets are only a "natural hedge" if you actually spend in USD. |
| **US estate tax — the one people miss** | As a non-US person, US-situs assets above **USD 60,000** (not indexed for inflation) are exposed to US estate tax at up to **40%** on death. Your US-listed ETFs (~USD 127,500) exceed that; the amount above the exemption is ~**USD 67,500**, and the potential tax at 40% is up to ~**USD 27,000 (≈HKD 213,000)**. **Mitigation:** consider Irish-domiciled UCITS equivalents (e.g. **CSPX** for S&P 500, **VUAA**, **VWRA**) which sit outside US estate tax and suffer only 15% fund-level dividend withholding vs 30% for US-domiciled ETFs held by a Hong Kong resident (HK has **no** US tax treaty; W-8BEN does not reduce the 30%). This is a real, quantifiable cost saving — confirm with a tax adviser. |
| **Behavioural risk** | The most likely reason this plan fails is you selling in a crash. First-time investors systematically buy high and sell low. |
| **Broker/custody risk** | Use a large, regulated broker with SIPC protection (USD 500,000 limit) and confirm your account type and beneficiary designations. |

**Overall risk rating: SRI 5/7 — High.** Appropriate for a 10+ year horizon and high stated tolerance; **not** appropriate if the money might be needed in under 5 years. You may get back less than you invest.

## 7. Risk-management rules (write these down and follow them)

1. **Phase in over 6–12 months** — never lump-sum a first portfolio at CAPE ~40x.
2. **Cap QQQ at 10%** and keep VEA at 15% — do not let the growth tilt become the whole portfolio.
3. **Rebalance at ±5pp bands** — this mechanically forces you to sell winners and buy losers.
4. **Keep 12 months of living expenses outside this portfolio.** This is what lets you hold through a crash.
5. **No stop-losses.** For a 10+ year growth portfolio, stop-losses convert temporary volatility into permanent loss and lock in the worst outcome. Your protection is time horizon, diversification and cash buffer — not selling.
6. **Address US estate tax before you exceed USD 60,000** — decide US-domiciled vs Irish-domiciled ETFs deliberately.
7. **Write a one-page IPS** (investment policy statement): target weights, rebalancing rule, what you will do in a −30% year. Sign it. Read it during the next crash.

## 8. What would make me change this view

- CAPE falling materially (a real bear market) → accelerate the phasing-in schedule.
- Fed pivoting to cuts and inflation falling → increase BND, reduce SGOV.
- US mega-cap earnings growth breaking down relative to the rest of the market → raise VEA, lower QQQ.
- Any change to the USD/HKD peg regime → reassess currency exposure.
- Your circumstances changing (job loss, property purchase, family) → reassess horizon first, allocation second.
