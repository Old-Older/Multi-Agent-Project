# Your US Stock Plan — A Plain-English Introduction

**For:** a 25-year-old Hong Kong investor · **Amount:** HKD 1,000,000 · **Time frame:** 15+ years
**Simulated / illustrative only — not an offer and not personal advice.** Prepared 19 November 2026.

---

## 1. The short version

You have money you will not need for at least 15 years, you want it to grow, and you could watch it fall
30–40% and still hold on (or buy more). That is the right starting point for a **mostly-stock portfolio**:
about **85% in shares and 15% in safety** (bonds and cash), spread across the US and the rest of the world,
using cheap funds you buy once and check once a year.

## 2. What the plan is

Six "sleeves" — six buckets, each holding one kind of fund:

| # | Bucket | What it holds (simple version) | Weight | Of HKD 1,000,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | US core | The 500 biggest US companies (S&P 500) | 45% | HKD 450,000 |
| 2 | US breadth | A wider US / world fund, so you are not only in the biggest names | 10% | HKD 100,000 |
| 3 | US growth | The 100 biggest tech-heavy US companies (Nasdaq-100) | 15% | HKD 150,000 |
| 4 | Rest of the world | Companies outside the US (Europe, Japan, emerging markets) | 15% | HKD 150,000 |
| 5 | Safety net | Short-term US government bonds | 10% | HKD 100,000 |
| 6 | Cash | HKD/USD cash for currency and trading | 5% | HKD 50,000 |

All funds are **Irish-domiciled** (see section 5) and **accumulating** — they keep dividends inside the fund
and reinvest them, so you do nothing.

## 3. Why it is built this way

- **Mostly shares, because you have time.** Shares are bumpy year to year but have grown the most over long
  periods. With 15+ years and your own emergency savings set aside, you can ride out the bumps.
- **Mostly the US, but not only the US.** The US has been the best market for a long time, but it is expensive
  today: US shares trade at about 25 times last year's earnings versus a long-run average of about 15 (CAPE
  38.8 vs a median of 16.1). When you pay a high price, future returns tend to be lower. Vanguard's model
  expects roughly **4.8–6.8% a year** from US shares over the next decade versus **5.3–7.3%** from developed
  markets outside the US. So 15% goes abroad to buy cheaper earnings.
- **A cap on the tech bet.** The ten biggest companies are about 37% of the S&P 500 and about 46% of the
  Nasdaq-100 — a lot riding on a few names. Keeping the Nasdaq-100 bucket at 15% means one bad year in tech
  cannot sink the whole plan.
- **A safety net you can actually use.** The 10% bonds and 5% cash are not there to make money — they are there
  so that if the market falls 30%, you have money to **buy more**, which is what you said you would do.

## 4. What could go wrong — and what to do

**A 30–40% fall is normal.** It is not a sign the plan is broken; it is the price you pay for the higher
long-run return. Over 15 years, expect at least one fall of 30% or more, and it can take years to recover.

| What happens | What it means in dollars | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Market falls 30% | HKD 1,000,000 → about HKD 700,000 on paper | Nothing. Do not sell. |
| Market falls 40% | HKD 1,000,000 → about HKD 600,000 on paper | Use the bond/cash buckets to buy more |
| Tech falls hard | A 40% fall in tech would cost this plan roughly 20% | Expected — that is why tech is capped at 15% |
| HKD/USD moves | Small: the peg keeps HKD between 7.75 and 7.85 per USD | Nothing |

**The biggest real risk is you.** Selling in a panic turns a temporary paper loss into a permanent one. If you
think you might sell, tell us now and we will lower the share weight before you start.

Other things to know: US share prices are high, so returns may be lower than in the past; the US government
keeps changing rules on AI chips and could change tax rules; and no one can promise a return.

## 5. What it costs, and the tax and estate points

**Costs (approximate).** The main fund charges about **0.07% a year** (HKD 700 on HKD 1,000,000). All-in,
including the tax the fund pays on dividends, the plan costs roughly **0.25% a year** — about HKD 2,500.
Changing HKD into USD once at a low-cost broker costs about **0.01%** (roughly HKD 100) instead of about 0.4%
at a bank (roughly HKD 4,000).

**Tax, in one paragraph.** Hong Kong does not tax you on investment gains or foreign dividends. The only tax
is inside the fund: the US takes 15% of the dividends the fund receives. If you bought US-listed funds
directly, the US would take **30%** of your dividends, because Hong Kong has no tax treaty with the US. The
Irish funds save you roughly **0.14% a year** — small, but it compounds over 15 years.

**Estate tax, in one paragraph.** If you died holding **US-listed** funds, the US could charge estate tax on
anything above **USD 60,000**, at up to **40%**. On this amount that could be roughly HKD 130,000–210,000
(approximate). **Irish-domiciled funds are not US assets**, so this bill is **zero** — the single biggest
reason this plan uses Irish funds.

## 6. The three things you must do

1. **Open a broker account.** Choose one that lets Hong Kong clients buy **Irish funds listed in London**
   (Interactive Brokers is the usual choice) and check it holds an SFC licence. Note: these funds are **not
   SFC-authorised** and are **not covered** by Hong Kong's investor compensation fund.
2. **Change HKD into USD once.** Do it in one go at the broker's own currency service, not at a bank, and not
   trade by trade. This alone can save you about HKD 3,900.
3. **Buy in 2–3 steps over about 3 months.** Buying all at once is fine, but spreading it out means you are not
   putting everything in on one bad day.

**Then:** check the plan once a year (and after any fall of 20% or more). If a bucket drifts more than 5
percentage points from its target, bring it back.

## 7. What to expect

If the plan earns about **5–7% a year** in USD, HKD 1,000,000 could become roughly **HKD 2.1–2.8 million in 15
years** before inflation. That is a range, not a promise — and a bad year of −30% to −40% along the way is
normal.
