**Prepared:** 19 November 2026 · Market data as of 17–18 Nov 2026 unless stated · **Simulated / illustrative — not an offer or personal advice. Not SFC-authorised products.**

## 1. Objective and suitability record
Grow HKD 1,000,000 over 15+ years with a globally diversified, low-cost, US-centred equity portfolio, sized so that a 30–40% drawdown is survivable and re-investable.
**Suitability record (KYC):** horizon 15+ years; growth objective; stated drawdown tolerance 30–40% with intent to hold or buy more; emergency savings held separately in HKD; no leverage, no individual stocks, no ESG constraint. **Explicit caveat: a 2000–02-style bear market would exceed this stated tolerance (see §4.2).**

## 3. Why this allocation
- **Equity-heavy fits a 15+ year horizon** with separate emergency savings; bonds are a damper and rebalancing reserve, not a return engine.
- **US core, not US-only.** US valuations are high: CAPE 38.76 vs median 16.14, trailing P/E 25.04 vs median 15.08 (multpl.com, 17 Nov 2026). Expected 10-yr US returns are only 4.8–6.8% (Vanguard VCMM, model run 30 Sep 2026, published 21 Oct 2026) vs 5.3–7.3% for developed ex-US; Goldman Sachs' 10-yr S&P 500 base case is 6.5% (range 3–10%, 12 Nov 2025). *JPMorgan's LTCMA was not retrieved — this is two houses, not three.* A 15% ex-US sleeve buys cheaper earnings (VXUS 10.8x forward vs VOO 17.6x; forward P/E is indicative, methodology undisclosed).
- **Concentration is the main hidden risk.** VOO's own top-10 holdings = 37.3% of the fund, QQQ 46.2%, VGT 64.0%, SMH 69.0% (stockanalysis.com, 17–18 Nov 2026). NVDA alone is 7.6% of VOO, 6.4% of QQQ, 17.3% of SMH. *(The S&P 500 index-level top-10 weight could not be verified and is not quoted.)* Anthropic is due to join the Nasdaq-100 on 21 Dec 2026 under the fast-entry rule, forcing index funds to buy it.
- **Irish-domiciled UCITS wrapper.** 15% US dividend withholding inside the fund vs 30% for a HK resident holding US-domiciled ETFs (no US–HK tax treaty) — **both rates unverified against primary sources**; net saving ≈0.13–0.14% p.a. on an assumed ~1.1–1.2% dividend yield (**approximate, unverified**). No US estate-tax exposure (see §4.6).
- **10% bonds + 5% cash** gives the client the ability to buy more in a −30% market, which is what they said they would do.

## 4. Risks
**4.1 Valuation / expected-return risk.** High starting multiples imply lower forward returns. CAPE 38.8 is in the top ~2% of history (only 1999–2000 and 2021 higher). This is a headwind, not a timing signal — Vanguard warns valuations are poor predictors over short or intermediate periods.
**4.2 Drawdown risk — quantified.** A 30–40% portfolio fall is a *normal* event over 15 years, not a tail. Live 2026 evidence: VOO −6.3%, QQQ −14.1%, VGT −18.6%, SMH −31.5% from their highs (incl. −11.2% in one day on 5 Nov 2026); 2025 intra-year S&P 500 drawdown −10.2% (multpl.com). Stress tests on this portfolio:
- −35% US equity / −25% ex-US / 0% bonds ≈ **−30%** (≈HKD 700,000).
- 2000–02-style (−49% S&P 500, −83% Nasdaq-100) ≈ **−45%** (≈HKD 550,000) — **this exceeds the client's stated 30–40% tolerance.**
Historical recovery times of 2–5+ years are **approximate/unverified**.
**4.3 Concentration / AI-cycle risk.** A single-stock AI disappointment transmits straight into "diversified" index funds via the 45% S&P 500 core (NVDA 7.6%) and the 15% Nasdaq-100 sleeve.
**4.4 Currency.** The HKD/USD peg (band 7.75–7.85, **unverified**) limits long-run FX trend risk; residual risk is the ±1.3% band plus broker spread. The 5% HKD cash sleeve is the only unhedged HKD buffer.
**4.5 Policy and rates.** The Nov 2026 US interim rule requiring licences for advanced AI-chip exports to data centres in several countries is a direct revenue risk for NVDA/AMD/AVGO and, via the supply chain, TSMC/SK hynix. Higher-for-longer rates are a headwind for long-duration growth stocks and for bonds (AGG −0.6%, BND −0.7% over 1 year).
**4.6 Tax and estate.** A HK resident holding US-domiciled ETFs faces 30% dividend withholding and US estate tax above a USD 60,000 exemption. Worked example: USD 128,000 in VOO → taxable ≈ USD 68,000 → ≈ **USD 17,000–27,000 (≈HKD 130,000–210,000)** at graduated 26–40% rates (**rate schedule unverified**; 40% is the top marginal rate, not the effective rate). Irish UCITS shares are foreign-corporation shares → **USD 0** US estate tax. No HK capital gains tax. The Investor Compensation Fund (HKD 500,000, **unverified**) does **not** cover LSE- or US-listed ETFs.
**4.7 Liquidity, tracking and counterparty.** LSE-listed UCITS lines have wider spreads than US primary-market ETFs; fund returns differ from the index by tracking difference, not just TER. Broker/custody risk: use an SFC Type 1-licensed broker and check the SFC public register.
**4.8 Inflation.** All return figures in §6 are nominal; at ~2–3% inflation the real return is roughly 2–4% p.a.
**4.9 Behavioural risk — live test.** The client's "hold or buy more" answer is being tested *now* (SMH −31.5%). Pre-commitment rule: rebalance into equities when the equity sleeve is >5pp underweight; do not check the portfolio daily.
**No leverage, no derivatives, no individual stocks** are used in this proposal.

## 5. Implementation
- Convert HKD→USD once at a low-cost broker (broker FX ~0.01% vs bank ~0.4%: ≈HKD 100 vs ≈HKD 4,000 — **approximate, unverified**).
- Buy in 2–3 tranches over ~3 months to reduce timing risk.
- Rebalance annually or when a sleeve drifts >5pp; rebalance into equities after a >20% fall.
- Review annually and after any 20%+ drawdown.

## 6. What to expect
At 5–7% nominal USD p.a., HKD 1,000,000 ≈ **HKD 2.1–2.8m in 15 years** — *before* inflation (~2–3% p.a.) and before the ~0.25% p.a. cost drag, and *below* the ~10% historical US average. A −30% to −40% year is normal and is the price of that return.
