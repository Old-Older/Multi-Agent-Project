# Semiconductors in 2027 — the plain-English version

**For:** colleagues and clients who want the story without the jargon. A one-page summary of the fund team's December 2026 deck; every number below appears in that deck. Not personal advice.

## What happened in 2026
The chip industry nearly doubled: sales reached **$1.51 trillion, up 89.9%**. Almost all of it came from one thing — **memory** (the chips that store data), up **249.5% to $803.9 billion**, or **53%** of the whole industry. Without memory, the rest grew **24.8%**.

So 2026 was not a boom in how many chips the world bought. It was a **price shock**: the same chips cost far more. Prices that jump that fast tend to come back down.

## Why 2027 is different
Growth slows to about **27%** (roughly **$1.9 trillion**), and the market splits:
- **Data-centre memory stays tight** through 2027; the newest high-bandwidth memory is expected to get much more expensive.
- **The cheaper kind of memory (NAND) starts to ease** in the second half of 2027 — spot prices already fell **20% in two weeks** in November.
- **Ordinary buyers are pulling back.** Laptop sales fell **9.4%** in 2026, and the parts cost of a new iPhone rose about **38%**, mostly because of memory. High prices are now taxing the customers who fund the boom.

## What we own and what we avoid
Share prices have already fallen: the main chip index is **34.1%** below its June 2026 high and trades at **13.6 times** expected earnings — cheap against its own history. But cheap only stays cheap if next year's profit forecasts hold.

We want to own the **"toll roads"** — businesses paid for every chip made, whatever the price: **chip factories and advanced packaging**, **chip-making equipment**, and **AI networking and custom chips**. These three are **overweight only on condition**: we hold them at **half the normal active weight** until we have verified how much of the advanced-chip market the main factory holds and how much packaging and high-bandwidth-memory capacity exists. If that check fails, they go back to neutral. We would rather own less of a good idea than assert a number we could not verify.

We are **neutral** on the biggest AI chip designer and on data-centre memory (we would trim memory into strength), and we **avoid NAND memory** (prices already falling) and **consumer, analog and auto chips** (their customers are cutting back).

Overall: **neutral to modestly overweight** chips, bought after the fall rather than at the June high. We size the position assuming it could halve — this index fell **87.2%** between 2000 and 2008.

## Three things that would change our mind
1. **Data-centre spending plans cut** — if the big cloud companies guide 2027 spending growth below **30%**.
2. **Memory prices turn down** — if contract prices for standard memory go negative.
3. **A shock we cannot price** — a broad new tariff (the proposed "Phase 2") or a Taiwan/cross-Strait event.

Any one of these and we would move to underweight.

**The one-line version:** 2026 was a memory price cycle, not a demand miracle. 2027 is slower and more selective — and a low share price is only a bargain if the profits hold.
