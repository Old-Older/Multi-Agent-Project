You buy in over 6 months (about HKD 150,000–200,000 a month), not all at once. Fund costs ≈ 0.05% a year, about HKD 500 on HKD 1m. The 5% cash stays after the 6 months: it is your cushion and rebalancing money, not a leftover.

## Three reasons for this shape
1. **Time is on your side.** With 10+ years you can accept short-term ups and downs for higher long-run growth. Your biggest advantage is not stock-picking skill — it is decades of compounding.
2. **Spread out, so no single bet sinks you.** The US market is dominated by a few tech giants. Adding smaller US companies, non-US markets and bonds means one bad area cannot wreck the plan.
3. **Simple, cheap and boring on purpose.** A few low-cost funds you can explain in one sentence beat a complicated portfolio you abandon in a bad year.

## Three main risks
1. **Prices are high today.** US shares are expensive versus history, so the next decade may return less than average and you could see a 20–30% fall in a normal bad year. That is why we buy in gradually.
2. **A big fall will happen.** How deep depends on the kind of bad year:
   - **Normal bad year: −20% to −30%** (HKD 1,000,000 → about HKD 700,000–800,000).
   - **Recession: −35% to −45%** (about HKD 550,000–650,000).
   - **Systemic crisis (2008-style): −45% to −55%** (about HKD 450,000–550,000), with recovery measured in years.

   These are on-paper falls, not permanent losses unless you sell. If the worst line would make you sell, lower the share weight *before* investing.
3. **The biggest risk is you, not the market.** Panic-selling locks in losses. Also: 30% US dividend withholding tax for non-US investors, **US estate tax** (below), and broker/custody risk — use a regulated broker.

## Two things Hong Kong investors must know
- **Currency (the HKD–USD peg).** The Hong Kong dollar is pegged to the US dollar (about 7.75–7.85), so day-to-day currency swings on US assets are removed for you. But the peg is a **policy regime, not a guarantee** — it can be tested or changed, and Hong Kong also imports US interest-rate policy. Treat USD as your main working currency, not as risk-free.
- **US estate tax.** If you hold US-listed funds or shares directly, US estate tax can apply to the part above **USD 60,000** for a non-US person — potentially up to 40% of that excess before your family receives anything. **Mitigation:** hold **Ireland-domiciled UCITS ETFs** instead of US-listed ones — e.g. **CSPX or VUAA** (S&P 500), **IWDA** (developed markets), **EIMI** (emerging markets), **AGGU or IB01** (bonds / T-bills). They are not US-situs assets, so US estate tax does not apply, and Irish funds usually suffer only **15% US dividend withholding** on US shares versus **30%** on a US-listed fund held by a non-US person. Trade-off: slightly higher fees and wider spreads. Confirm with a qualified tax adviser before any real money.
