## 3. Why this allocation — the reasoning

**1. You are 25, so time is your biggest asset.** A 15–20 year horizon means a 40% drawdown is a *temporary* mark-to-market event, not a permanent loss — provided you don't sell. Historically, US equities have never delivered a negative 20-year real return. This justifies an equity-heavy portfolio (93% growth assets).

**2. Broad core first, opinions second.** 83% of the money sits in index funds. This is deliberate: stock-picking is a *hobby-sized* allocation (10%), not the engine. If all eight stocks went to zero, you would lose 10% — annoying, not fatal.

**3. Diversify away from the concentration you're forced to own.** Buying the S&P 500 today means ~37% in ten companies, mostly AI-linked tech. So:
- **VXUS (15%)** gives you Europe, Japan and emerging markets — Goldman is overweight Japan for 2027 (+24% expected).
- **SCHD (8%)** tilts to cash-generative value stocks, which historically hold up better when rates rise.
- **AVUV (5%)** adds the small-cap value premium, which is negatively correlated with mega-cap growth.
- **UNH, JPM, COST** in the satellite add healthcare, financials and consumer staples.

**4. The growth tilt is intentional but capped.** QQQM (10%) plus the AI-linked stocks means roughly **25–28% of the portfolio is in the growth/tech theme**. That is a deliberate bet that AI capex continues — but capped so a capex bust costs you a quarter, not half, of the portfolio.

**5. Cash is a feature, not laziness.** 7% in T-bills yields ~3.8–4% risk-free, gives you buying power if the December rate hike triggers a deeper selloff, and — most importantly — stops you from panic-selling equities at the bottom.

**6. Costs are near-zero.** Blended ETF expense ratio ≈ **0.06%**. On HKD 1m that is ~HKD 600/year. This is the single most reliable "alpha" available to you.

## 5. Risks — the honest list

### Market risk (the big one)
A 15%-volatility equity portfolio **will** fall 20%+ at some point. That is normal, not a failure.

| Scenario | Portfolio impact | Value of HKD 1,000,000 |
|---|---|---|
| Mild correction (−10%) | −HKD 100,000 | HKD 900,000 |
| 2022-style rate shock (−25%) | −HKD 250,000 | HKD 750,000 |
| AI-capex bust, tech-led (−30%) | −HKD 300,000 | HKD 700,000 |
| 2020 COVID crash (−34%) | −HKD 340,000 | HKD 660,000 |
| 2008 GFC (−51%) | −HKD 510,000 | HKD 490,000 |

**You must be able to look at HKD 490,000 in your account and do nothing.** If you can't, reduce the equity weight now, not later.

### Concentration risk
~37% of the S&P 500 is ten stocks, mostly AI-linked. Your portfolio's effective exposure to the AI theme is ~25–28%. A single disappointing hyperscaler capex guide could hit a quarter of your money at once.

### Valuation & rate risk
Forward P/E of 17.6× is reasonable, but the **10-year Treasury at ~5.1%** means bonds now offer a real alternative. If the Fed hikes in December *and* January, multiples could compress further even if earnings hold up.

### Currency risk — smaller than you'd think
The HKD is **pegged to USD at 7.75–7.85** under the Linked Exchange Rate System. So your USD assets have **almost no FX risk against your HKD spending** — a rare advantage. The real risk is the *opposite*: if the peg were ever changed (low probability, high impact), your USD assets would actually protect you.

### Tax risk — this is the one people miss
- **Dividend withholding: 30%.** Hong Kong has **no US tax treaty**, so the US withholds 30% of every dividend, versus 15% for treaty countries. On a ~1.3% yield that is a **~0.39%/year drag** (~HKD 3,900/yr).
  - *Mitigation:* **Irish-domiciled UCITS equivalents** (e.g. CSPX/VUAA for S&P 500, or an accumulating version) suffer only 15% inside the fund and are outside US estate tax. Saving ≈ **HKD 1,950/year** plus estate-tax protection. Trade-off: slightly higher expense ratio (~0.07%) and wider spreads. **For a HKD 1m portfolio this is worth doing for the core ETFs.**
- **US estate tax: the serious issue.** Non-US persons get only a **USD 60,000 exemption** on US-situs assets, with rates up to **40%**. Your US-situs holdings would be ~**USD 119,000** → roughly **USD 59,000 above the exemption → up to ~USD 23,700 (≈HKD 185,000)** of potential estate tax exposure. Unlikely at 25, but it is a real structural flaw.
  - *Mitigation:* hold the **core in Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs** (not US-situs), keep only the satellite stocks in US-listed form, and/or use a corporate/trust structure if the portfolio grows large. **This is the single highest-value structural fix in this proposal.**

### Behavioural risk
The biggest threat to your returns is you selling in a panic. See §7.

### Other
Geopolitical (Iran conflict, energy prices), regulatory (US tech antitrust), single-stock blowups (any of the 8 could halve), and liquidity (fine for these names, but small-cap AVUV can gap in stress).

## 6. Guardrails (rules to write down and follow)

1. **Max single stock: 2%** of portfolio. Never add to a loser beyond that.
2. **Max single sector: 35%** (currently tech is ~30% — at the limit).
3. **Cash floor: 5%** at all times.
4. **Rebalance when a holding drifts ±3 percentage points** from target, or once a year, whichever comes first. Rebalance by directing new money first, selling only if necessary.
5. **No leverage, no options, no inverse/thematic ETFs** in this portfolio.
6. **Review quarterly; do not check daily.** Daily checking correlates with worse behaviour.
7. **Emergency fund separate.** Never fund living expenses from this portfolio.

## 8. When this proposal would be wrong

- **If this HKD 1m is your entire net worth** → keep 6 months of expenses in HKD cash first; invest the rest.
- **If you need the money within 5 years** (property down payment, education, business) → cut equity to 40–50% and add short-duration bonds.
- **If a 40% drawdown would make you sell** → cut equity to 60% and add bonds. A portfolio you can hold beats a better portfolio you abandon.
- **If you want income now** → shift SCHD/VXUS up and growth down; but at 25 this is usually a mistake.
