# Semiconductors: Client Talking Points (end-2026)

## 1. Why chips matter, and why the story is now AI datacenters

Semiconductors are the basic building blocks of almost everything electronic — phones, cars, factories, medical equipment and the internet itself. Global chip sales are expected to reach about $1.51 trillion in 2026, up roughly 90%, and to grow to about $1.9 trillion in 2027. What has changed is where that growth comes from: it is now mostly AI datacenters, where large numbers of chips work together to train and run AI models. Memory is now more than half of the whole chip market, and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) — the fast memory AI systems need — is sold out through 2027. The big cloud companies are expected to spend about $785 billion on datacenters in 2026, rising toward $1 trillion or more in 2027, though that spending growth is expected to slow sharply in 2028.

## 2. Three questions clients are likely to ask

**"Isn't this a bubble after the November selloff?"**
It is a fair question. On 5 November 2026 the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 12.25% in a single day, and AI-related stocks have lost roughly $13 trillion of value from their highs. That is a real repricing of expectations, not a rounding error. But a sharp fall and a bubble are not the same thing: the companies are still selling real products at real prices, and HBM is sold out through 2027. What the selloff tells us is that prices had run ahead of certainty, and that this sector can fall hard and fast. We treat it as a higher-risk holding, sized accordingly, not as a one-way bet.

**"Why own chips if memory prices are so high?"**
High memory prices are a sign of shortage, not a reason to avoid the sector — but they cut both ways. Today they help memory makers' profits and they raise costs for everyone buying chips. The risk is that high prices encourage new supply, and prices can fall as fast as they rose. That is why we prefer a broad exposure across the industry rather than a concentrated bet on memory alone, and why we watch pricing trends closely.

**"What about China?"**
China is both a large customer and a competitor, and export controls can change the picture quickly in either direction. We do not try to predict policy. We keep China exposure as one part of a global portfolio, and we accept that headlines about restrictions can move prices sharply.

## 3. What we will NOT claim

- We will not promise any return, or say this sector "must" go up.
- We will not call the top or bottom, or tell you when to buy or sell.
- We will not claim to know how the AI build-out looks after 2027 — spending growth is already expected to slow sharply in 2028.
- We will not present a single cost figure (for example, TSMC's 2nm wafers at roughly $30,000 each) as a forecast of profit or share price.
- We will not say a past selloff makes the sector "cheap" or "safe".
