Executive summary

Five judgments for 2027

The industry enters 2027 with record revenue, record margins and record capex — and a share price that has already begun to discount the end of the cycle.

  1. The upcycle is intact on fundamentals, not on price. WSTS put 2026 industry revenue at ~US$975bn (+26%); NVIDIA guides Q4 FY27 to ~$130bn (+90% y/y) and TSMC raised FY26 growth to ~44%. Yet the SOX fell ~24% in Oct–Nov 2026, its worst two months since 2008.
  2. Memory is the tightest part of the chain and the most cyclical. SK hynix earned an 80% operating margin in Q3 2026; HBM4 prices are forecast to rise from $2.05/Gb (2026) to $3.00–5.50/Gb (2027). TrendForce sees 2028 as the turning point.
  3. Bottleneck value is migrating from the transistor to the package and the memory stack. CoWoS is still ~10% short at end-2026; HBM will absorb ~30% of the big three's DRAM wafer input.
  4. China is a shrinking revenue pool but a rising supply competitor. NVIDIA's China accelerator shipments fell 44% y/y; domestic vendors hold 28% share; China is ~30–41% of global WFE.
  5. Positioning: own the bottleneck, underweight the beta. We prefer foundry/packaging, HBM leaders and leading-edge equipment over broad index exposure, sized for a drawdown of the kind already seen in 2026 (the SOX fell ~24% in Oct–Nov).

Bottom line: the debate for 2027 is not whether AI demand is real — the order books say it is — but whether the capex that produces it earns a return. We are constructive on earnings, neutral on multiples, and we hold memory with a written exit trigger.

What the numbers say

Read-through: this is the classic late-cycle signature — fundamentals still accelerating while the multiple contracts. It rewards stock selection and punishes index beta.

Deep dive 1

Memory: the best margins in the industry, and the shortest fuse

Pricing

HBM4: $2.05/Gb (2026) → $3.00–5.50/Gb (2027); HBM ASP +122% in 2027.

Conventional DRAM +10–15% q/q and NAND +15–20% q/q in 4Q26.

2026 was an HBM "pricing pause"; the surge is a 2027 event.

Profitability

SK hynix Q3 2026: revenue KRW 104.6tn (+328% y/y), operating profit KRW 83.3tn, 80% margin.

Samsung Q3 2026 guidance: operating profit ~KRW 107.4tn (+782% y/y).

Micron FQ4 2026 operating margin 80.7%.

Supply

HBM to take ~30% of the big three's DRAM wafer input.

CXMT capacity growth limited to ~20%; China gaining in cheaper memory.

Goldman/TrendForce expert call: DRAM shortage could last until 2028.

Why we still own it

  • HBM is largely contracted rather than spot: long-term agreements fix much of 2027 volume and price in advance.
  • HBM4 qualification is a technical barrier — Samsung may retake #1 in 2027, which is a share shift, not a price collapse.
  • Every dated 2026 datapoint shows undersupply through 2027–28.

Why we cap it

  • 80% margins are a signal to competitors, not a steady state. Record 2026 memory capex is being committed now.
  • TrendForce names 2028 as the turning point; the equity will discount it 6–9 months earlier.
  • Consumer end-demand is weak — the upcycle is AI-only, so the downside is not cushioned by a broad recovery.

Deep dive 2

China and export controls: a smaller revenue pool, a stronger competitor

The policy regime at end-2026

  • BIS remote-access rule (19 Nov 2026): data centres anywhere supplying significant compute to China-HQ'd firms need licences.
  • Policy looser on sales, quieter on enforcement: licensed H200 sales for a 25% fee; no Entity List additions since Oct 2025; Blackwell and Rubin still barred.
  • Section 232: 25% chip tariff effective 15 Jan 2026, with a 90-day expansion review.
  • "Build-or-pay": US production avoids the tariff; ~$1.2trn of committed US investment cited.

China's response

  • NVIDIA China accelerator shipments −44% y/y in Q3 2026; Huawei and domestic vendors at 28% share.
  • SMIC Q3 2026: revenue $3.19bn (+33.9%), net profit +123.4%, 97.3% utilisation; only 7nm-class foundry.
  • Self-sufficiency ~30% by 2026 (from ~16% in 2020); CXMT revenue +600% y/y.
  • China WFE ~$50bn, 30–41% of global WFE (estimates conflict).

Investment implication: treat China revenue as an option, not a base case. NVIDIA's Q4 guide already assumes zero China data-centre compute. Equipment makers are de-risking fastest — AMAT China 25% of revenue (from 28.9%), Lam 35% (from 43%), ASML 23% of system sales.

Portfolio construction rules

  • Cap single-name exposure at 8% of the sleeve — concentration, not volatility, is the real risk here.
  • Cap memory at 20% of the sleeve and hold it with a written exit trigger (see next slide). This cap applies wherever memory is held, including the HBM leaders named on slide 5.
  • Staged entry: the SOX is already −24% from its June peak; deploy in three tranches over 3–6 months rather than one ticket.
  • Hedge the tail, not the beta: Taiwan exposure is a binary risk that options price cheaply in calm periods.
  • Benchmark awareness: an index-relative mandate cannot be underweight the top five without tracking-error budget (the permitted deviation from the benchmark) — agree that budget with risk before implementing.

Trade-off we are accepting: we give up some upside if the bull case runs, in exchange for a defined exit in memory and lower single-name concentration. We judge that trade worth making at ~16x forward (indicative, secondary source) with 80% margins in the supply chain.

Six numbers that tell us the cycle has turned

1. Hyperscaler capex guide

Current: ~$720–745bn for 2026, +77% y/y.

Trigger: any top-4 company guides 2027 below 2026 actual.

2. HBM4 contract price

Current: $2.05/Gb (2026), forecast $3.00–5.50/Gb (2027).

Trigger: settles below $3/Gb, or 2027 ASP growth below +50%.

3. DRAM contract price q/q

Current: +10–15% in 4Q26.

Trigger: two consecutive quarters of flat-to-negative prints.

4. CoWoS supply-demand gap

Current: ~10% short at end-2026, ~170k wpm in 2027.

Trigger: gap closes to zero — packaging stops being scarce.

5. WFE run-rate

Current: $143.9bn 2026F; Lam sees >$200bn in 2027.

Trigger: SEMI cuts the following-year forecast two vintages in a row.

Trigger: above ~22x without an earnings upgrade — that is where we trim.

Falsification

What would make us wrong

We would be wrong on the upside if…

  • AI inference revenue at the hyperscalers grows faster than depreciation, restoring free cash flow and allowing another capex step-up.
  • HBM4 qualification slips at one supplier, extending the shortage into 2028 and pushing prices above $5.50/Gb.
  • China reopens: licensed H200 volumes resume at scale and NVIDIA's zero-China assumption becomes upside.

We would be wrong on the downside if…

  • Memory capex committed in 2026 arrives as supply in 2027 rather than 2028.
  • Custom ASICs take share faster than expected, compressing merchant GPU pricing.
  • A Taiwan disruption or a broader tariff regime makes the whole chain uninvestable regardless of earnings.
