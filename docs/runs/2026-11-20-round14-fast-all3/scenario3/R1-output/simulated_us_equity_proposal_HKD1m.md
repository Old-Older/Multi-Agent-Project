## 1. Plain-language introduction (read this first)

You are 25. You have HKD 1,000,000 (about **USD 127,450** at USD/HKD 7.8465, 18 Nov 2026). You have 15–20 years before you need this money, and you have told me you can watch it fall 40–50% without selling. That combination — young, long horizon, high tolerance — is the single most valuable thing you own as an investor, and this plan is built to use it.

**The idea in one paragraph.** Most of your money goes into a cheap fund that owns hundreds of big US companies. Over the last 100 years US shares have returned about **10% a year** on average (S&P 500, 1926–2025, nominal, CIBC Wood Gundy table 2026) — but **do not plan on 10%**. Today's valuations are high, so the honest planning number for this portfolio is **5–7% a year**, with a wide error bar. A smaller slice goes into areas the big index is *light* on — smaller companies and non-US companies — so your money is not all riding on the same few giants. A small slice (about 15%) goes into 10 individual companies you can name and follow, capped at 5% each, so you learn how owning a business feels. And 7% stays in cash so you never have to sell at the worst moment. **This is a simulation for learning — not advice, not an offer, and no return is promised.**

**What you must accept.** This portfolio can lose half its value. It has happened before: the S&P 500 fell **−49% in 2000–02** and **−57% in 2007–09**; the Nasdaq fell **−77% to −83%** in 2000–02 and took about **15 years** to get back to its peak (Boldin, 11 Sep 2025; DailyFX drawdown calculator and 5i Research, both undated — figures are widely reported but not verified against a primary source). On HKD 1,000,000 that is a paper loss of HKD 400,000–500,000, and you would need a **+67% to +100%** gain just to get back to even. If that sentence makes you want to sell, this plan is too aggressive for you and we should cut the equity weight.

**Two costs you pay no matter what.** (1) US dividends paid to a Hong Kong resident are taxed **30%** at source (no US–HK tax treaty). (2) US-listed assets above **USD 60,000** can be exposed to **US estate tax of up to 40%** for a non-US person — your ~USD 127,000 is about double that threshold (Bogleheads NRA table, 20 Jun 2025; Chambers Private Wealth 2025; Taxes for Expats, 28 Sep 2026). These are certain, not hypothetical, and they are why §7 asks you to speak to a tax adviser before signing anything.

**Is the market expensive right now? Two measures disagree.** The S&P 500's **forward P/E** (price divided by the next 12 months' expected earnings) is **17.7x** (FactSet, 5 Nov 2026) — *below* its 5-year average of 19.8x, because earnings estimates rose 33.6% this year while the index rose only 7.4%. But the **Shiller CAPE** (price divided by 10 years of inflation-adjusted earnings, which smooths out booms and busts) is **~40x** — the second-highest in about 150 years, behind only 44.2 in December 1999 (AInvest 12 Sep 2026; MoneyLion 9 Nov 2026; eToro 22 Oct 2026). Translation: the market is cheap *if* the AI-driven profit boom lasts, and expensive *if* it does not. Nobody knows which. That is exactly why we diversify and enter in stages rather than betting everything on one view.

**What to do next.** Read §2–§5 and the tax points above, then decide: (a) do you accept the −50% stress case in writing, (b) do you have separate emergency savings (6 months of expenses) outside this HKD 1,000,000, and (c) have you checked the tax/estate points with an adviser? If yes to all three, start the staged entry in §6. If not, tell me and we will lower the equity weight.

## 3. Why this allocation (the reasoning, sleeve by sleeve)

**1. 45% VOO — the core.** You want US stocks; the cheapest, broadest way to own them is an S&P 500 index fund at a 0.03% fee. Long-run evidence: ~10%/yr nominal over 1926–2025 (CIBC Wood Gundy table, 2026). A commonly cited claim is that every rolling 20-year window since 1926 has been positive on a total-return basis — **I could not verify this against a primary source, so treat it as unverified**; the defensible point is that a 15–20 year horizon is long enough for US equities' historical compounding to have outweighed past drawdowns. At a 15–20 year horizon, this is the highest-probability way to compound. It is also the sleeve that makes the plan cheap: fees of 0.03% versus 1%+ for an actively managed fund is roughly 1% a year of return you keep.

**2. 15% QQQ — a deliberate growth tilt, not a diversifier.** QQQ overlaps about **53%** with VOO and is roughly **80% technology** (third-party ETF snippets — UNVERIFIED, confirm on issuer pages). It is here because you are 25 and can absorb volatility in exchange for growth exposure, and because Nasdaq-100 forward P/E is reported at **18.7–19.2x** (XTB 12 & 17 Nov 2026; AInvest 7 Nov 2026) — below its own long-run −2 standard deviation band (i.e. cheaper than its own history despite the rally; this is a third-party estimate from XTB, 12 Nov 2026, not verified against a primary source). It is *not* here to spread risk; it concentrates it. That is why it is 15%, not 30%.

**3. 10% AVUV — small-cap value.** The S&P 500 is market-cap weighted, so it is dominated by the largest companies. Small-cap value has historically earned roughly **+1.5–2.0%/yr** over large caps (Fama-French research, 1926–2025 — a directional estimate, not a verified precise figure), and it is the part of the US market least correlated with mega-cap tech. This is the sleeve that pays you when the AI trade cools.

**4. 8% VXUS — non-US.** US equities have beaten ex-US markedly over 2010–2025 (S&P 500 ~13–14%/yr vs MSCI EAFE ~5–6%/yr), but long-run returns are broadly similar and relative performance runs in multi-decade cycles. An 8% sleeve is small — deliberately, because you asked for US stocks — but it means a US-specific shock (policy, dollar, tech regulation) does not hit 100% of your portfolio.

**5. 15% individual stocks — capped conviction.** Ten names, 5% each, across six sectors. The cap is the point: no single company can cost you more than 5% of your capital, and the sector spread (tech, financials, healthcare, energy, industrials) means one bad industry does not sink the sleeve. This sleeve is also where you learn — following ten businesses through a cycle teaches more than any article.

**6. 7% cash — the behavioural insurance.** HKD 70,000 in HKD money market / short T-bills. Two jobs: it funds the staged entry in §6, and it means a job loss or an emergency never forces you to sell shares at the bottom. At a 3.75–4.00% Fed funds rate (FOMC, 28 Oct 2026), cash also pays something while it waits.

**Why not 100% US mega-cap tech?** Because the S&P 500's top-10 holdings were already roughly **40–43%** of the index in 2026 snapshots (ATB Financial 28 Jan 2026; AInvest Jul–Oct 2026), vs an 18–23% average in 1990–2015 — a dated snapshot, not a live figure. A "diversified" index fund is now substantially a bet on seven or eight companies. Adding QQQ and mega-cap stocks on top would push your effective exposure to those same companies well above 50%. The sleeves above are chosen to *reduce* that overlap, not add to it.

**Expected return, honestly stated.** Do not plan on 10%/yr. With CAPE near 40x, high-valuation periods have historically been followed by roughly **2–5%/yr real** over the next 10–15 years. A reasonable planning range for this portfolio is **5–7%/yr nominal** with a wide error bar. At 6%/yr, HKD 1,000,000 becomes about HKD 3.2m in 20 years — before inflation. That is the honest case, not the exciting one.

## 5. Rules that make the plan work

1. **Position caps:** single stock ≤5% at purchase, trim above 7%; single issuer ≤10% including ETF look-through; tech ≤45%; non-US sleeve ≥5%.
2. **Rebalancing:** review semi-annually; rebalance when any sleeve drifts >5 percentage points from target; trim any stock above 7% immediately. Mechanical, not discretionary.
3. **Cash:** 7% (HKD 70,000) in HKD money market / T-bills, ideally equal to 6 months of living expenses.
4. **No leverage, no margin, no options writing.** Hard rule.
5. **Written investment policy statement** signed by you, including the pre-commitment not to sell below −40% without a 30-day cooling-off period.
6. **Annual review** of tax/estate position and platform/custody arrangements.
