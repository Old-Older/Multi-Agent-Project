# Who sets the AI rules in your course? Inside HKUST's course-by-course GenAI regime

*A news report for HKUST students and faculty. Claims are sourced at the end; items marked [unverified] must be checked on the live page before publication.*

## What happened — and why it matters

**HKUST has no single university-wide rule on whether you may use generative AI in assessed work.** The change is a **shift in where the decision sits**: from the university to the individual course. Under the **"Policy for GenAI Integration in Teaching and Learning"** (Center for Education Innovation, CEI), **each instructor sets the rule for their own course** and must **communicate it in writing** for courses offered from **Fall 2023-24 onward**. CEI's policy page says:

> "Given the evolving landscape of GenAI tools, HKUST allows faculty members the flexibility to set their own course-level policies. Faculty members are required to communicate the chosen policy option to students in writing for courses offered starting Fall 2023-24." [unverified — page could not be opened directly; quoted from the indexed official page]

The clearest example of a unit going **further than the framework requires** is PPOL. Its Summer 2025-26 PPOL5480 syllabus says improper or fabricated references "have become such commonplace forms of academic misconduct in the written assignments of students, that as of Spring Term 2025-2026, it is now the official policy of the Division of Public Policy (PPOL) that the use of AI…" [unverified — the sentence is truncated in the indexed copy]. The same syllabus warns that "academic misconduct need not be intentional for it to qualify as academic misconduct" and that "ultimately, it is you and not AI that will be held accountable."

**Why this matters:** because the university delegates the decision, **two students in the same term can face opposite rules** — and you are judged by the rule written in your course, not the one a friend in another department heard. The answer to "can I use AI here?" is in **your own syllabus / Canvas site**; we found no central per-course GenAI register. If a syllabus is silent, ask the instructor in writing; silence is not permission.

## Is GenAI allowed — and must you declare it?

**Declared?** There is **no university-wide declaration requirement that we could find**: we saw "you must give proper credit" language in some courses and explicit prohibition in others, but **no evidence of a single HKUST-wide rule requiring every student to declare GenAI use in every course**. Whether you must declare is a course-level question. [unverified — absence of evidence, not evidence of absence]

**What happens if you get it wrong?** Students are bound by the **Academic Honor Code**, adopted by the Senate in **June 2005**; under the misconduct procedure described in the UGC Quality Assurance Council's review of HKUST (**August 2025**), cases may be handled locally or referred to the **Senate Student Disciplinary Committee**, with sanctions from written reprimand to suspension of rights or required training. **HKUST does not appear to publish AI-specific misconduct case numbers**, so "AI cheating on the rise" claims cannot be sourced. [unverified — absence of evidence]

## The wider picture

A peer-reviewed survey of **680 HKUST students** in the *Journal of Asian Public Policy* (online **3 May 2025**; co-authored by **Sean McMinn, Director of CEI**) reports that **44.71% of respondents used ChatGPT at least five times** and **91.69% intend to keep using it as a learning tool**. In **April 2025** HKUST announced that **HKGAI V1**, which it describes as Hong Kong's first homegrown large language model, would be free to all staff and students, and the **Library ran a chatbot beta pilot from 6 May to end-June 2025**; no usage figures have been published for either. A **mandatory AI Common Core** of **six credits of AI and data literacy for every undergraduate admitted from 2026-27 onward**, including **AISC 1000 "Foundations of AI Literacy: Human-AI Orchestration"**, is a **2026-27 commitment** that does not alter the rules for students already enrolled.

## What to do next

**Students**
1. **Check your own course first** — the GenAI section in the syllabus and on Canvas is the rule you are held to. **If it is unclear or missing, ask the instructor in writing** and keep the reply.
2. **Declare and credit where your course requires it** — some courses require proper credit for any GenAI use; treat that as a graded requirement.
3. **Use the support that exists:** the Library's **"AI at the Library"** hub (library.hkust.edu.hk/ai-library), its **"AI Tools for Academic Research"** mini-course and **AI Literacy FAQs** (guidance on disclosing AI use), and **ITSO's Generative AI Usage Guidelines** (itso.hkust.edu.hk → Generative AI tools → Guidelines).
4. **Know the process:** academic-integrity rules and the misconduct procedure are published by the **Academic Registry**; appeals go to the Provost **within 14 days** of receiving the decision.

**Faculty**
1. **State your course's GenAI rule in writing, in the syllabus and on Canvas**, as the policy requires — and be specific about which assessed components it covers (exams vs. projects, drafting vs. final text).
2. **Get help designing the rule and the assessment:** CEI's **Generative AI in Education** guidelines and its **AI Sandbox for Educators** workshops; the Library's AI hub for information-literacy approaches.
3. **Consider the equity clause.** The CEI policy frames course-level choices around **equitable access** to AI tools — a rule assuming every student has a paid subscription disadvantages some of them. [unverified — the full sentence is truncated in the indexed text]
