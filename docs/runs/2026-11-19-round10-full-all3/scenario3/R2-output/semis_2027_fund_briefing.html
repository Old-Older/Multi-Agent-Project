Executive summary

Five judgments we are asking the committee to adopt

Each is a position, not a forecast of the cycle — and each has a stated trigger that would reverse it.

1 · The cycle has split in two

  • HBM/AI memory is sold out to 2029 with >50% of 2027–28 capacity under long-term agreements.
  • Commodity DRAM contract momentum is decelerating toward flat in Q4 2026; NAND is forecast into surplus in 2H27.
  • Treat "memory" as two businesses with different exit multiples, not one cycle.

2 · Valuation is not the bear case — earnings durability is

  • SOX forward P/E 13.6× vs a post-2011 median of 15.7×: the index has already de-rated 34% from its June 2026 record.
  • Micron at 4.0× and SK Hynix at 2.3× forward earnings are not cheap — they are the market pricing peak-cycle EPS.

3 · The binding constraint moved from wafers to packaging

  • CoWoS capacity ~120–140k wpm end-2026 vs ~13k end-2023 — a ~10× expansion, still sold out.
  • TSMC's own chairman: packaging "limits my customers' growth". A16 slipped to 2027.

4 · Concentration makes this an index event, not a sector event

  • S&P 500 top-10 weight 37.9%; NVIDIA alone ~7.5%; effective N of 47 vs 106 in 2019.
  • An AI-capex disappointment transmits through every balanced portfolio we run.

5 · Position for a barbell, not a beta trade

  • Own the bottleneck (packaging, leading-edge foundry, HBM with contracted revenue) and own the cash-return compounders; underweight the middle — merchant GPU at peak margins and commodity memory at peak spreads.
  • Size the position to survive a −34% drawdown, because the index delivered one in the last five months.

Why the 2027 deceleration matters more than the 2026 record

  • Growth of +90% → +27% is the arithmetic of a price cycle rolling over, not of demand stopping.
  • Memory is forecast to grow +32% in 2027 vs ~+250% in 2026 — the second derivative turns negative first.

  • Demand destruction is already visible at the edges: 2Q26 smartphone shipments −7.4% y/y as supply is reallocated to server DRAM/HBM.

The bull case is now a volume argument

  • Goldman Sachs: agentic AI drives a 24× increase in token consumption by 2030, with unit token costs falling — margin upside for hyperscalers, volume upside for silicon.
  • Goldman also expects a chip shortage for the next 12–18 months as capacity catches up.

The bear case is a funding-quality argument

  • Capex is increasingly debt-, partnership- and equity-funded; only Microsoft among the largest spenders is still self-funding.
  • 2027 consensus of ~$935bn is only ~7% above the Q4-2026 annualised run-rate of ~$874bn — the 2027 story is the current pace held, not an acceleration.
  • Company language for 2027 is directional only ("increase significantly", "meaningfully more") — no hard guidance until February 2027.

Our read: the demand is real, but it is now a credit-sensitive demand. A 10% cut to 2027 capex plans removes roughly $90–130bn of silicon revenue — more than the entire 2025 memory market.

The supply side

The ceiling is the assembly line, not the fab

Whoever owns the bottleneck owns the pricing power — and in 2026 that is advanced packaging and HBM, not wafers.

Advanced packaging: a 10× expansion that is still sold out

120–140k wpm
CoWoS capacity, end-2026 (vs ~13k at end-2023; ~170k expected 2027)
  • TSMC chairman C.C. Wei (Q2 2026 call): packaging capacity "is so tight that now it limits my customers' growth"; CoWoS "sold out" through 2026.
  • TSMC 2026 capex $52–56bn, of which 10–20% to advanced packaging and masks — the clearest signal of where incremental AI dollars go.
  • NVIDIA reportedly reserves ~60% of CoWoS output — a structural advantage for the incumbent.
Inside Deep Tech (16 Nov 2026); TrendForce (16 Apr 2026); Troy Technical (20 Jun 2026); Manufacturing Mag (13 May 2026).

Leading edge: on schedule, but geographically trapped

  • N2 in high-volume manufacturing since Q4 2025; 8% of Q3 2026 wafer revenue (from 3% in Q2) — a fast ramp by historical standards.
  • A16 slipped to 2027; N2 is made only in Taiwan under the N-2 rule; N2-family capacity to more than double in 2027.
  • Arizona: Fab 21 Phase 1 running; Phase 2 (N3) pulled forward to H2 2027; total US commitment raised to $265bn.
  • TSMC Q3 2026: revenue $46.72bn (+50.9% y/y), gross margin 66.4% — pricing power intact.
Inside Deep Tech (19 Oct 2026); Data Center Knowledge (16 Jul 2026); TSMC 3Q26 management report.

Investment implication

Supply tightness is good for 2027 earnings and bad for 2028 multiples. Every dollar of capex announced today becomes capacity in 2028–29. The right question for a fund manager is not "is demand strong?" but "how much of this tightness is already in the price of the equipment and foundry names that sell into it?"

The cycle question

One industry, two cycles — and they are diverging

The single most important analytical decision in this deck: stop treating memory as one asset class.

Cycle A — HBM / AI memory: still tight

  • SK Hynix: DRAM/HBM effectively sold out to 2029; 14 long-term-agreement customers (up from 10); LTAs cover >50% of 2027–28 capacity.
  • TrendForce raised 2027 blended HBM ASP forecast to +121% y/y (HBM4 mix); HBM bit supply +50–60%, still short of demand.
  • Share (Q2 2026): SK Hynix ~50%, Micron ~18%, Samsung gaining; all three qualified for NVIDIA Vera Rubin HBM4.
  • Per-stack pricing: HBM3 ~$200 → HBM3E ~$300 → HBM4 ~$550.
TrendForce (28 Oct 2026, 29 Sep 2026, 4 Aug 2026); Counterpoint via Silicon Analysts (Nov 2026).

Cycle B — Commodity DRAM / NAND: rolling over

  • Conventional DRAM contract price: +45–50% q/q (4Q25) → +93–98% (1Q26) → +13–18% (3Q26) → ~0–5% (4Q26).
  • Sources disagree on Q4 (+0–5% vs +10–15%) — the disagreement itself is the signal that the momentum trade is over.
  • TrendForce expects NAND surplus in 2H27 — "well before DRAM". The upcycle has a visible end-date.
  • Luminix (30 Sep 2026): the data crossed its July threshold for re-rating the "early-peak" scenario.
TrendForce via NextMSC (9 Oct 2026); Luminix (30 Sep 2026).

What this means

Peak-cycle margins are already in the numbers: SK Hynix posted an 80% operating margin in Q3 2026 and Micron an 87% gross margin. Those are not sustainable levels for a commodity-linked industry — which is exactly why the market pays 2.3× and 4.0× forward earnings for them. The asymmetry is in HBM contract coverage, not in the memory label.

Why this changes how we size the position

  • Our balanced and global equity mandates already carry double-digit effective semiconductor exposure through index weights. Any explicit sector position is additive, not diversifying.
  • The historical drawdown distribution is brutal: SOX worst calendar year −48.0% (2008); deepest peak-to-trough −87.2% (Mar 2000–Nov 2008).
  • 2026 already delivered a −34% drawdown inside a positive year. Position sizing must assume that repeats.
  • Correlation risk: memory, GPU and equipment names now share one driver — hyperscaler capex — so "diversifying across the stack" diversifies less than it used to.
History of Market (18 Nov 2026).

Practical rule for the committee

Measure semiconductor exposure at the total-portfolio level (direct + index-embedded), set a hard cap, and treat any single-name position above 2% of NAV as a concentrated bet requiring an explicit thesis review. The 2026 round trip is the stress test to use.

Geopolitics & policy

A managed two-way valve — priced as a tail risk, not a base case

2026 policy did not decouple the industry; it rationed it. That is more investable, and more volatile, than either extreme.

What actually happened in 2026

  • H200 to China: conditional exports to approved customers with a 25% US revenue share; case-by-case licensing from Jan 2026; ~10 Chinese firms cleared by May; first meaningful deliveries Jul–Aug 2026.
  • Still banned: H100/H800, A100 and Blackwell B100/B200/GB200 remain under presumption of denial. H20 is effectively dead in China (US$4.5bn charge).
  • NVIDIA guides zero China data-centre compute for Q4 FY27 — China is now upside, not base case.
  • Beijing counter-controls: NDRC per-purchase approvals and Hong Kong routing requirements; import approvals deliberately slowed to protect domestic suppliers.
EliteAI.tools compilation (12 Nov 2026); GPUSmith (26 Jul 2026); NVIDIA Q3 FY27 release (17 Nov 2026).

The structural shift: geography as a moat

  • Taiwan's N-2 rule keeps the newest node at home: N2 is Taiwan-only, while Arizona gets N3 (Phase 2, H2 2027) — a permanent one-node lag offshore.
  • TSMC's US commitment raised to $265bn; offshore fabs carry higher cost and lower margin — a slow structural drag on group gross margin.
  • Huawei projected at up to $12bn of China AI-chip revenue in 2026, heading toward 50–60% domestic share — the export valve is building its own replacement.
  • Pending US legislation (Chip Security Act, GAIN AI Act) would add location-verification and US-buyer priority — more compliance cost, more policy beta.
Inside Deep Tech (19 Oct 2026); Data Center Knowledge (16 Jul 2026); GPUSmith citing FT (26 Jul 2026).

What we could not verify — and therefore do not put in the numbers

Section 232 semiconductor tariff rate and scope, 2026 Entity List changes, SMIC/Cambricon 2026 capacity data, China equipment self-sufficiency ratios, and EU Chips Act / Rapidus / Korea / India progress were not sourced in this research round. They are treated as unquantified tail risks in the scenario work that follows, not as inputs to the base case.

Three paths, one dominant variable: capex funding

Capex grows ~20–28% to $0.9–1.0tn, then plateaus. HBM tight, commodity DRAM flat-to-down from 1H27, NAND surplus in 2H27. Memory EPS peaks and de-rates further; foundry and packaging hold. Bear
25%

Why base is 50%, not 70%

The 2027 consensus is only ~7% above the current annualised run-rate — the market is already assuming a plateau. That makes the base case less of an upside surprise and more of a "nothing changes" outcome, which is precisely the environment in which peak-cycle margins get tested.

What we are NOT forecasting

We are not calling an AI bubble burst. The evidence for a 2000-style collapse (SOX −87% peak-to-trough) requires a funding event we cannot yet see. We are calling a de-rating plus an earnings reset in the most cyclical parts of the stack — which is enough to matter for a portfolio.

Investment implications

A barbell: own the bottleneck and the cash returners, avoid the peak-margin middle

Illustrative positioning framework for discussion — not a trade instruction. Weights are relative to a semiconductor sleeve, not to total NAV.

Overweight — the bottleneck

  • Leading-edge foundry & packaging: the constraint is CoWoS and N2, and pricing power sits there. TSMC at 17.4× with 66.4% gross margin is the cleanest expression.
  • HBM with contracted revenue: SK Hynix (2.3×) and Micron (4.0×) are priced for a collapse that LTAs covering >50% of 2027–28 capacity argue against. Size for volatility, not for comfort.
  • Custom-ASIC enablers: Broadcom at 20.3× carries the growth premium, but ASIC share of HBM bit demand is rising — the mix shift is real.

Underweight / avoid — the peak-margin middle

  • Commodity DRAM/NAND exposure without LTA cover: contract momentum is already ~flat; NAND surplus is forecast for 2H27.
  • Merchant GPU at peak margins: NVIDIA's 74.8% gross margin and 10.4× multiple both assume the current cadence continues; zero China revenue is already the base case, so there is no policy upside left in the number.
  • Equipment at cycle-peak order books: ASML at 22.1× is a great company bought at a capex-cycle top — wait for the order-book reset rather than pay for it.

Implementation

Build in tranches over 2–3 quarters; keep dry powder for the 15–25% drawdown the base case assumes. Rebalance on capex guidance, not on price.

Hedges

The cheapest hedge against a capex pause is not shorting semis — it is trimming index-embedded exposure and holding cash/short duration against the concentration risk in §8.

What we would buy on a bear

A capex pause that leaves HBM LTAs intact is a buying opportunity in foundry and HBM, not an exit. Pre-commit the levels now, while the analysis is calm.
