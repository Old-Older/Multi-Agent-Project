01 · Where we stand

2026 was a memory super-cycle wrapped around an equity de-rating

What happened to the industry

  • Global semi revenue $1,655bn in 2026, ~+108% YoY; H1 alone was $702bn (+102%). WSTS, 13 Aug 2026.
  • Memory +302% in 2026; logic only +45%. The headline is a price story, not a unit story.
  • 2027 WSTS: ~$2.1tn, ~+29% — i.e. growth decelerates by two-thirds in one year.
  • Equipment: SEMI sees total tool spend $165.9bn in 2026 (+23.2%) and a record $229bn by 2028.

What happened to the equities

  • SOX peaked 14,655 on 22 Jun 2026, fell −38.5% to the 5 Nov close (worst day −12.2%), then rallied to +36.3% YTD by 19 Nov.
  • Forward P/E fell from 26.1x to ~13x — lowest since Jan 2019 — because consensus EPS rose 155%, not because prices collapsed alone.
  • SOX trades at 13x vs S&P 500 at 17.8x, on 2026–28E sales CAGR of 40.2% vs 8.7%.
  • ~$6.5tn of market cap was lost from the 30 SOX names at the trough (Livewire Markets, 16 Nov 2026).

Interpretation: this is an earnings-led de-rating, not a bubble deflating. That is a more constructive setup than 2000 — but it also means the entire valuation case rests on the EPS ramp being delivered.

Sources: WSTS press release 13 Aug 2026; SEMI Mid-Year forecast 14 Jul 2026 (via Canadian Manufacturing); MarketWatch via Morningstar 6 Nov 2026 (FactSet consensus); History of Market 19 Nov 2026; Livewire Markets 16 Nov 2026.

03 · Demand

Memory: still rising, but the rate of change has peaked

Pricing

  • Q4 2026 conventional DRAM contract +10–15% QoQ — decelerating from far larger earlier moves.
  • NAND +15–20% QoQ, with enterprise SSD the only accelerating category.
  • TrendForce still sees undersupply into 2027; BofA/TrendForce see HBM prices roughly doubling by 2027 UNVERIFIED.

HBM4 — the execution risk

  • HBM4 mass production slipped twice: end-1Q26 → 3Q26 (NVIDIA raised per-pin spec >11 Gbps; certification issues).
  • SK hynix HBM revenue share 58% → 50% (1Q→2Q26); Samsung 21% → 33%.
  • NVIDIA is evaluating tiered HBM configs for Rubin Ultra to cut memory content per chip — a direct threat to HBM bit demand (DIGITIMES, 18 Oct 2026).

Why this matters for the portfolio

Late-cycle signature. DRAM price growth decelerating while NAND accelerates, and consumer end-markets (PC, gaming) already cutting — that is the classic shape of a memory peak forming, not a mid-cycle pause.

Share is the alpha, not the price. With HBM4 qualification deciding 2027 earnings, the memory trade has become a single-name execution bet. We prefer to own it through the equipment/packaging layer that sells to whoever wins.

Watch item: if HBM4 pricing is set at the 3Q26 certification, Samsung's share gain compresses SK hynix's 2027 EPS even with flat ASPs.

Sources: TrendForce 30 Sep 2026; DRAMeXchange 7 Jan 2026; Chosunbiz citing TrendForce/Counterpoint 21 Oct 2026; DIGITIMES 18 Oct 2026; BofA/TrendForce webinar via KuCoin 14 Nov 2026 (UNVERIFIED).

04 · Supply

The bottleneck has moved from wafers to packaging and lithography

Foundry & capex

  • TSMC Q3 2026 revenue +41.2% YoY, FY26 ~+44%; HPC = 68% of revenue.
  • 2026 capex guided $52–56bn, ceiling raised to $64bn (Jul 2026); 2027 sell-side range $67–80bn (Citi 67 / Goldman 78) — no company guide exists.
  • Five N2 fabs ramping in 2026 (~60k wpm); N2 capacity >+100% YoY in 2027; CEO: supply still short in 2027.
  • CoWoS is the customer default; TSMC extending to 9X/14X reticle packages.

Equipment

  • SEMI: total equipment $165.9bn 2026 (+23.2%), WFE $143.9bn; 300mm fab equipment $151bn in 2027, first year above $150bn.
  • ASML Q3 2026 record €11.66bn sales, 57.5% GM; FY26 €44–45bn, China ~20% of FY26 sales; says 2027–28 capacity will fall short of demand.
  • China share of toolmaker revenue fell everywhere YoY: ASML 42%→23%, Lam 43%→35%, KLA 39.5%→34.5%, TEL 40.3%→36.4%, AMAT 28.9%→25.4%.

Investment read: scarcity pricing sits with whoever owns the constrained step — advanced packaging, EUV/low-NA lithography, and the deposition/etch steps that scale with layer count. That is where pricing power and backlog visibility are highest, and where a demand wobble hits last.

05 · Policy

Export controls moved from "deny" to "license for a fee" — and China is localising fast but shallow

US policy, as it stands

  • AI Diffusion Rule unenforced since May 2025; formal rescission reported alongside the new remote-access rule (17 Nov 2026) UNVERIFIED.
  • H200 / MI325X to China: case-by-case licensing plus a 25% fee/tariff from 14 Jan 2026. Blackwell/Rubin still licensed.
  • Section 232: 25% tariff on certain advanced computing chips from 15 Jan 2026; most HS 8542 ICs remain duty-free.
  • BIS closed the subsidiary loophole (31 May 2026) and restricted Chinese entities' remote access to US AI chips via offshore data centres (17 Nov 2026).
  • Binary date: 10 Jan 2027 — affiliates-rule suspension and the Busan truce expire.

China's substitution curve

  • Domestic equipment: ~23% of new-tool spend, but ~35% of installed units, up 10pp YoY.
  • Weak spots: implant and inspection/metrology below 15%; EUV effectively nil.
  • SMIC/Huawei at 7nm-class DUV multi-patterning; no verified 5nm volume. Ascend is mainly an inference part.
  • CXMT is the world's #4 DRAM maker at ~7–8% share.

Read: China can substitute mature-node and inference silicon; it cannot yet scale leading-edge AI silicon. The revenue risk to Western vendors is a policy risk (Beijing guidance, remote-access rule), not a capability risk.

Sources: EMSNow 12 Nov 2026; Baker McKenzie 17 Nov 2026; HK TID Circular 67/2026 15 Jan 2026; Findchips 11 Nov 2026; Jiwei/JW Insights via ETNews 15 Sep 2026; Pandaily 3 Sep 2026; Tech Times 28 Aug 2026; Zenodo 30 Oct 2026. China IC self-sufficiency %: UNVERIFIED.

07 · The central debate

Is the AI capex cycle financeable into 2027–28?

The bull case

  • NVIDIA Q3 +103% YoY and Q4 guided +12% QoQ with zero China — demand is not the constraint.
  • TrendForce still sees DRAM undersupply into 2027; ASML says 2027–28 tool capacity falls short of demand.
  • 13x forward P/E on 40% sales CAGR is the cheapest the group has been since 2019.
  • 2026 capex: Microsoft $170bn, Amazon ~$220bn, Meta $135–145bn, Alphabet $51.3bn in Q3 alone.

The bear case

  • Funding, not demand, is the binding constraint. Alphabet has posted two consecutive negative-FCF quarters; BofA sees the four hyperscalers' combined FCF swinging to −$180bn in 2027 from +$240bn in 2024 UNVERIFIED (BofA via AInvest, 3 Nov 2026).
  • Microsoft trimmed 2026 capex $175bn → $170bn on GPU-as-a-Service delays — the first hard pushout.
  • Strategists are converging on 2027 as the capex peak (Dan Ives, 5 Nov 2026; Steve Eisman, 15 Nov 2026).
  • NVIDIA's gross margin guided down to 71–72% as Vera Rubin ramps — the cost curve is turning up.

Our judgment: 2027 is likely the peak rate of growth in AI capex, not the peak in absolute spend. That distinction matters: absolute spend can rise while multiples compress, which is exactly the 2026 pattern repeating. We position for revenue durability, not for the growth rate.

Sources: AInvest 3 Nov 2026 (citing Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon, BofA); Benzinga 5 Nov 2026; Yahoo Finance 15 Nov 2026; NVIDIA 18 Nov 2026; TrendForce 30 Sep 2026; Quartr ASML 14 Oct 2026.

08 · Investment implications

Four positions we would hold into 2027–2030

1 · Own the bottleneck (overweight)

Advanced packaging, EUV/low-NA lithography, and deposition/etch. Scarcity pricing, backlog visibility, and the last place a demand wobble shows up. This is the core of the book.

2 · Own the ASIC shift (overweight)

Custom-XPU design houses and the foundry/packaging that builds them. ASICs take 58.8% of 2027 accelerator units (derived) vs NVIDIA's 35.1%. Custom XPUs are already 73% of one large vendor's AI revenue — the mix shift is real, not forecast.

3 · Memory: tactical, not structural (neutral)

Pricing still rising but decelerating; HBM4 share is a single-name execution bet. Prefer equipment exposure to memory capex over memory ASP beta. Trim into strength.

4 · Consumer / mature-node (underweight)

Gaming −40% YoY, PC demand weakening on component costs, and China localisation biting at mature nodes. This is where the memory bill is being paid.

Sizing: sector-level views only — no formal single-name buy list. Indicative risk budget: bottleneck basket 40–50%, ASIC-shift sleeve 20–25%, memory sleeve capped at 10% and treated as a trading position, remainder cash/short-duration. Review dates: after Q4 2026 results (Feb 2027) and after the 10 Jan 2027 policy date.

10 · Conclusion

Three things to take into 2027

01

The cycle is late, not over

Memory price growth is decelerating and consumer demand is cracking, but leading-edge supply stays short through 2028. Late-cycle is a positioning problem, not an exit signal.

02

Value has moved to the bottleneck

Packaging, lithography and the ASIC supply chain capture the scarcity rent. Merchant-GPU leadership is intact in dollars but eroding in units.

03

The risk is the earnings, not the multiple

At 13x, the multiple is not the problem — the +155% EPS ramp is. Position for revenue durability and pre-commit to buying the −45% tranche.
