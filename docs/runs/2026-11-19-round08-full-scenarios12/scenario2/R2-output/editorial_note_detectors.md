# Editorial note — Fact-check: AI-writing detectors

**Claim checked:** "AI-writing detectors can reliably determine whether a university student's assignment was written by AI."
**Verdict:** FALSE as stated (not "detectors are useless" — they are not reliable enough to decide misconduct alone).
**Audience:** students and faculty. **Length:** ~1,080 words of body text (sections 1–5), 1,324 words including the sources list.

**Editorial judgments**
- We interpreted "reliably" as an assessment standard: low false positives and low false negatives on real student work, across languages and subjects. That is the standard the article tests.
- We lead with the strongest verified evidence (peer-reviewed abstracts, vendor's own admissions) and keep vendor figures labelled as self-reported. The article also states that no independent audit of any vendor figure exists — the strongest single support for the verdict.
- We deliberately do not print the widely circulated "61% of non-native essays flagged" figure as a finding: it is secondary (HEPI) and the paper's full text was not read. It is disclosed in the article's limitations section (§5, "What we could not confirm").
- The biggest limitation is stated in the article itself: no verified peer-reviewed study on real student assignments or non-English writing. The verdict rests on abstract-level and synthetic-text evidence.

**Open items for a follow-up (selective list — the article's §5 is the complete list of limitations)**
1. Read the full texts of Liang et al. and Weber-Wulff et al. (publisher paywall/bot blocks). For Liang, the missing item is the Results-section numeric false-positive rates (TOEFL-essay vs US-student essays). For Weber-Wulff, the missing item is the overall accuracy figure and the verbatim conclusion from the body — the article quotes only the abstract.
2. Find a 2024–2026 peer-reviewed evaluation on real student assignments, and one on non-English or mixed-language writing.
3. Read the OIA case summaries themselves (July 2025); the article currently relies on HEPI's account of them, and no individual wrongful-accusation case has been verified from a primary source.
4. Verify Turnitin's 2024–2025 model changes and any newer published accuracy figure.
5. Verify Hong Kong-specific data on detector use (UGC, CUHK), which was snippet-only, and check for any HKUST-specific detector dispute (none confirmed).
