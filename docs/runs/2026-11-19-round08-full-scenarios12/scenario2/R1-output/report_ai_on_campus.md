# AI on HKUST Campuses: The Rules Are in Your Syllabus, Not in One Rulebook

**A news report for HKUST students and faculty**
*Draft v2 — revised after fact-check. Quotations are verbatim from the sources listed at the end; items we could not verify are labelled as such in the text.*

---

## The story in one paragraph

Since Fall 2023-24, HKUST's central AI rule for coursework has not been a single university-wide rule: **each instructor decides how much generative AI (GenAI) is allowed in their own course, and must tell students in writing.** Alongside that, the university has documented a protected GenAI chatbot for all staff and students, a HK$10 million teaching fund, a supercomputer that students can reach only through a supervising principal investigator, and a Von Neumann Institute inaugurated on 9 May 2025 at a ceremony addressed by the Financial Secretary. That combination — a decentralised rule sitting on top of a fast-expanding AI infrastructure — is what students and faculty are living with this year, and it is why two students in the same semester can face completely different rules.

---

## What happened: the policy, in plain terms

**1. The rule is set course by course.** HKUST's Centre for Education Innovation (CEI) states: *"Given the evolving landscape of GenAI tools, HKUST allows faculty members the flexibility to set their own course-level policies. Faculty members are required to communicate the chosen policy option to students in writing for courses offered starting Fall 2023-24."* CEI says the framework was *"formulated in consultation with faculty members and students from both undergraduate and postgraduate courses."*

**2. There are three options.** When the guidelines were first issued in March 2023, HKUST's School of Engineering described them as offering faculty the flexibility *"to allow students' use of generative AI in assignments freely or conditionally, both with acknowledgement, or to ban its use, according to the needs of individual courses."* In the same article HKUST stated: *"HKUST has become the first university in Hong Kong to embrace generative AI and recognize its potential to transform education."* That is HKUST's own claim about its timing, not an independent ranking.

**3. The rule is supposed to appear in the syllabus.** The Academic Registry's assessment policy (page dated 1 August 2025) says *"the course syllabus should be devised to present course ILOs, assessment components, grading rubrics, and relevant policies like the use of Generative AI tools and the rules of academic integrity clearly and make it accessible to students at the start of the course via Canvas or other appropriate platform."*

**4. CEI tells faculty not to rely on AI detectors.** CEI's guidance reads: *"CEI believes that solely relying on AI detection tools to detect AI-generated content in students' output is not the best solution, as technology advances quickly and AI detection tools often lag the advancement of Generative AI."* It adds: *"Instead of focusing all efforts on finding a bulletproof AI detection tool, we recommend that you shift your focus to reviewing your assessment design."*

**5. The student-facing integrity page does not name AI — as far as we could read.** The Academic Registry's academic-integrity page states: *"Students are required to maintain the highest standards of academic integrity. The University has zero tolerance for academic misconduct."* It lists examples of misconduct, but in the text we retrieved it does not mention generative AI or AI detectors. **This is provisional:** the page may continue beyond what we could read, so we do not conclude that no AI guidance exists elsewhere in the Registry's materials. What we can say is that the AI-specific rule a student is most likely to be judged against is the one written into the course syllabus.

**6. Support has been documented since February 2023.** CEI ran faculty workshops from 17 February 2023 and a four-part online course series (GENAI-001 to GENAI-004) (undated page). A **HK$10 million EDGE-AI Fund**, launched in spring 2023, funded 13 projects in its first round, six of them from Engineering (School of Engineering, 22 September 2023). The university also provides a **secured GenAI chatbot platform to all staff and students**, built on Azure OpenAI, which CEI describes as addressing *"equitable access, including addressing geographical limitations and access to paid versus open-source tools."*

## What's coming next (2026)

These are later announcements, not part of the 2023–25 policy changes above.

- **A mandatory AI core for all incoming undergraduates.** HKUST announced on 14 July 2026: *"Beginning in the 2026/27 academic year, all incoming undergraduate students will be required to complete a six-credit AI common core program."* The first part is "Foundations of AI Literacy: Human–AI Orchestration".
- **An AI Literacy Hub**, launched with Solomon Learning Group — launch ceremony 16 October 2025, announced 7 November 2025 — aimed mainly at schools and community outreach.

## Why it matters

**For students: read your syllabus, not the rumour.** Because the rule is course-level, the practical questions — may I use AI to draft this essay? must I declare it? is it banned in the exam? — have different answers in different courses in the same semester. The syllabus is where the course-level rule is supposed to be stated, and the Registry's policy says it should be there at the start of the course. If it is silent or ambiguous, that is a question for the instructor.

**For students: detection is not CEI's recommended method.** CEI explicitly warns that AI detectors lag behind the technology and recommends assessment redesign instead. That is CEI's stated position; our research did not establish whether individual instructors or the Registry use detector scores in practice, so treat "the university won't detect it" as an assumption, not a fact.

**For faculty: the policy hands you the decision — and the work.** Course-level freedom is also course-level responsibility: writing the rule into the syllabus, explaining it, and — per CEI — reviewing assessment design so the rule is workable. The EDGE-AI Fund and CEI's assessment-design resources exist for this; the fund's first round funded 13 projects, six in Engineering.

**For everyone: AI infrastructure on campus has expanded alongside the rules.** In the same period HKUST stood up a supercomputing facility, SuperPOD (55 NVIDIA DGX H800 nodes — 440 H800 GPUs, a figure derived from ITSO's hardware spec page rather than an official GPU-count announcement — and 448 PFLOPS TF32), rolled out a fourth-generation HPC cluster (HPC4) in mid-October 2024, and inaugurated the Von Neumann Institute on 9 May 2025 to work on embodied intelligence, generative AI and advanced supercomputing. The university's annual report records 16 new research institutes, centres and joint labs with external partners in 2024-25, several AI-related (including joint labs with Alibaba, BYD, Inspur Cloud and Wuxi). Students' access to that compute is indirect: SuperPOD account applications are open to faculty as principal investigators and usage is charged, so a student reaches it through a supervising PI.

**The gap worth watching (our reading, not a finding).** A campus that already runs on GenAI — HKUST was, per its own researchers, *"among the first universities worldwide to introduce officially protected ChatGPT services"* — is governed by a decentralised rule that depends on every instructor writing it down. Whether that produces flexibility or inconsistency is the live question for this academic year. That is our assessment; no survey we found measures it.

## What we could not confirm

We are labelling these rather than filling them in:

- **How much students actually use AI at HKUST.** A university-wide survey (November 2023, 680 students across the Hong Kong and Guangzhou campuses, published in the *Journal of Asian Public Policy*, online 28 December 2024) reports *"significant usage of ChatGPT services among the students and a very high level of intention to continue that use"* and that *"The students perceive ChatGPT to contribute positively to both their learning and career development"*, with differences by gender, level of study, age, discipline and origin. We could not read the full text, so **we do not report specific usage percentages.**
- **A claim that HKUST students use AI daily more than students elsewhere** (attributed to a Digital Education Council survey) appeared only in a search snippet of that paper. **Unverified — not used as a finding here.**
- **No HKUST-specific AI-detection dispute, petition or exam-policy controversy** was confirmed in our research. We do not report one.
- **No official HKUST figures on GenAI-related misconduct cases** were found.
- **No HKUST faculty-attitude survey** was found, so the "For faculty" section above rests on official guidance, not on data about what faculty think.
- **No verified comparison** of HKUST's GenAI policy with HKU, CUHK or PolyU was completed, so this report makes no such comparison.
- **The March 2023 circular itself** is behind HKUST login; we rely on CEI's and the School of Engineering's descriptions of it.
- **The 440-GPU SuperPOD figure is derived** (55 nodes × 8 GPUs) from ITSO's spec page; no official GPU-count announcement was found.
- **HKUST(GZ) has a separate AI-use policy**; this report covers the Clear Water Bay campus.
