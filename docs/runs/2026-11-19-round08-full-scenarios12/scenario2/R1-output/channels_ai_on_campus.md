## Channel 1 — Campus social-media / bulletin post

**Headline: Your AI rule is in your syllabus — not in one university rulebook**

Since Fall 2023-24, HKUST has let each instructor decide how much generative AI is allowed in their own course, and faculty must tell students in writing. Two students in the same semester can be working under different rules.

**Key points**
- **Set course by course.** HKUST's Centre for Education Innovation (CEI) says faculty may allow GenAI "freely or conditionally, both with acknowledgement, or to ban its use, according to the needs of individual courses".
- **The syllabus is where the rule is supposed to be stated.** The Academic Registry's assessment policy says the syllabus should set out "relevant policies like the use of Generative AI tools and the rules of academic integrity".
- **Detectors are not CEI's recommended method.** CEI says "solely relying on AI detection tools" is "not the best solution" and recommends reviewing assessment design instead. Our research did not establish whether instructors or the Registry use detector scores in practice.
- **Support exists.** A HK$10 million EDGE-AI Fund funded 13 projects in its first round, and a secured GenAI chatbot platform is provided to all staff and students.

**What to do:** Check your course syllabus before you use AI in any assignment — and if the rule is silent or unclear, ask your instructor. Faculty: make sure your course policy is written down and visible from day one.

## Channel 2 — Three-minute spoken briefing script

*For a class or staff meeting. Approximate delivery time: 3 minutes.*

**[Opening hook — about 15 seconds]**

Two students sit in the same semester at HKUST. One may use generative AI to draft an essay, as long as they acknowledge it. The other may be banned from using it at all. Both are following the rules. The difference is which course they are in.

**[Section 1 — The rule is set course by course]**

HKUST's Centre for Education Innovation states that HKUST "allows faculty members the flexibility to set their own course-level policies", and that faculty "are required to communicate the chosen policy option to students in writing for courses offered starting Fall 2023-24". When the guidelines were first issued in March 2023, the School of Engineering described them as letting faculty allow GenAI use "freely or conditionally, both with acknowledgement, or to ban its use, according to the needs of individual courses". So there is no single campus-wide answer to "can I use AI here?" The syllabus is where the course-level rule is supposed to be stated. The Academic Registry's assessment policy says the syllabus should set out "relevant policies like the use of Generative AI tools and the rules of academic integrity", and be accessible at the start of the course. If it is silent or ambiguous, that is a question for the instructor.

**[Section 2 — What the university says about detection]**

CEI's guidance on AI detectors is blunt: "solely relying on AI detection tools to detect AI-generated content in students' output is not the best solution, as technology advances quickly and AI detection tools often lag the advancement of Generative AI." Its recommendation is to "shift your focus to reviewing your assessment design". That does not mean AI use is unregulated. It means the stated approach is to design assessments that make misuse harder, rather than to police output with a detector score. Note too that the Academic Registry's student-facing academic-integrity page states the university has "zero tolerance for academic misconduct", but in the text we retrieved it does not mention generative AI — a provisional finding, since the page may continue beyond what we could read. Our research did not establish whether instructors or the Registry use detector scores in practice.

**[Section 3 — The campus is changing fast]**

Meanwhile, the campus is being rebuilt around AI. HKUST provides a secured GenAI chatbot platform to all staff and students. A HK$10 million EDGE-AI Fund, launched in spring 2023, funded 13 projects in its first round, six of them from Engineering. The university stood up a supercomputing facility, SuperPOD — 55 NVIDIA DGX H800 nodes, which works out to 440 GPUs, a figure derived from the hardware spec page rather than an official GPU-count announcement — and rolled out a fourth-generation HPC cluster, HPC4, in mid-October 2024. On 9 May 2025 it inaugurated the Von Neumann Institute. Students reach that compute indirectly, through a supervising principal investigator. Separately, in 2026 HKUST announced a mandatory six-credit AI common core for all incoming undergraduates from 2026/27 — a later announcement, not part of the 2023–25 policy changes above.

**[Closing line]**

So the practical message is simple: the rule that applies to you is written in your course syllabus. Check it before you use AI — and if you teach, make sure it is there.
