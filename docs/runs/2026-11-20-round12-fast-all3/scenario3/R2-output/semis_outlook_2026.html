Semiconductors: The Future of the Industry

An end-2026 perspective for the fund team · data as of 19 Nov 2026

What this deck argues our view

  • The 2026 boom is a memory-price cycle, not a unit-volume cycle — the 2027–2030 view must be built on a memory price path.
  • Pricing power has rotated from the GPU to TSMC (foundry + CoWoS) and the HBM trio.
  • Valuation is no longer the main risk: SOX at 13.7x forward vs S&P 500 17.7x after a −38% drawdown. Earnings durability is now the bigger risk (see slide 8).
  • Concentration is the amplifier: semis 18.8% of the S&P 500 (record); NVDA ~20.6% of SMH.

The WSTS (+90%) vs Gartner (+64%) gap is ~$200bn on the same year; our reading is that the spread is mainly memory pricing (the fact sheets do not decompose it). Memory is forecast at ~+250% to >$800bn in 2026 (≈53% of the WSTS total) forecast. This is a price number, not a demand number.

2. The cycle: where in the up-cycle are we?

  • Accelerating evidence: hyperscaler 2026 capex commitments >$700bn (Janus Henderson, 30 Apr 2026); TSMC raised 2026 capex ~15% mid-year to $60–64bn (16 Aug 2026); TechInsights raised 2026 capex growth to +34% from +27%.
  • Decelerating evidence: DBS forecasts growth slowing to +24.7% in 2027 then −3.7% in 2028, with memory stocks −40–50% and SOX ~−30% (6–7 Aug 2026).
  • Memory relief not before 2028; "memflation" is suppressing non-AI hardware demand (Gartner, 7 Apr 2026).
  • HBM/HBM4 contract ASPs forecast +121% YoY for 2027 forecast, single source — if it lands, a classic peak-of-cycle signature.

Our judgment

Judgment: 2026 is the peak rate of change. Base case: 2027 still grows but decelerates sharply; the memory price line rolls over in 2028. The investable question is not "is AI real" but "how long does the memory price hold".

5. Concentration: the amplifier

  • Semis = 18.8% of S&P 500 market cap — a record, more than triple the 2022 share (Unusual Whales, 21 Jun 2026; Morgan Stanley ~18%, 30 Jun 2026).
  • Top-10 S&P names ≈ 40% of index market value (Morgan Stanley, Jul 2026).
  • SOX top-10 weight is not published; we use SMH as the investable proxy throughout.
  • SMH top-10 = 69.0% of a 26-stock portfolio; NVDA 20.64%, TSM 9.78%, AVGO 5.49% (Stock Analysis, 23 Oct 2026).
  • SMH AUM $66.4bn, TER 0.35% (VanEck, 30 Oct 2026). Past performance is not indicative of future returns.

Implication: a "diversified" semis allocation is in practice an NVDA + AI-capex bet. Position sizing, not stock selection, is the main risk control.

6. Investment implications

What we would own

  • Core: TSMC. The only player with simultaneous pricing power in leading-edge logic and advanced packaging; 44% USD growth with capex rising is a share-gain signal, not a warning.
  • Tactical: memory (SK hynix / Micron / Samsung). Own the 2027 ASP upgrade, but treat it as a cycle trade with a stop, not a compounder. Micron at 9.75x trailing is priced for a downturn — that is the asymmetry.
  • Structural: equipment/materials — benefits from $60–64bn TSMC capex and memory WFE +48%/+26% (2026/27) forecast.
  • Optionality: China domestic tools (NAURA, AMEC) — policy-driven share gain from a low base.

What we would not

  • Adding to a concentrated SMH position at 69% top-10 / 20.6% NVDA without a sizing rule.
  • Owning memory as a long-term compounder: the 2027 ASP upgrade is a cycle trade.
  • Underwriting 2029–2030 on 2026 memory prices. No verified 2029/2030 consensus exists — use scenario ranges.

Portfolio rules we propose our proposal, not sourced

Denominator = total fund NAV. Semis sleeve ≤ 15% of NAV; single-name ≤ 5% of NAV (TSMC is the core within the sleeve, not an exception to the cap); memory sub-sleeve ≤ 1/3 of the sleeve, exited on a −20% drawdown from cost or on two consecutive quarters of falling DRAM contract prices; build in three tranches over ~3 months; rebalance when the sleeve exceeds 18% or falls below 12% of NAV. Access rule: US-listed SMH/TSMC ADR by default; SK hynix/Samsung only via an approved QDII or Korea-access route. Factor netting: the sleeve is netted against existing NVDA/AI-capex exposure elsewhere in the fund. Fund-level drawdown budget: a −10% NAV drawdown triggers a mandatory sleeve review. Owner: PM, reviewed quarterly with risk.
