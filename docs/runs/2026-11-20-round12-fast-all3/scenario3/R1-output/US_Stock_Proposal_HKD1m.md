## 2. Why this allocation (the reasoning)
1. **Time is your biggest asset.** Over 1928–2025 US large caps returned **~10.0%/yr nominal** vs **4.5%** for 10-yr Treasuries and **3.4%** for T-bills (Damodaran, NYU Stern; figures via secondary citations — primary table not opened). HKD 1m at ~7%/yr for 20 years ≈ HKD 3.9m; at 3.4% ≈ HKD 1.95m. That gap is the whole point of owning shares.
2. **Core first, satellites second.** 55% in VOO+VTI is a low-cost base (0.03% fees). Tilts (QQQM, AVUV, SMH = 25%) are sized so a 50% fall in the riskiest sleeve costs ~2.5% of the portfolio.
3. **Valuation is below average on one measure, not cheap.** S&P 500 forward P/E **17.7x (FactSet, 5 Nov 2026)** vs 5-yr 19.8x / 10-yr 19.1x (FactSet, confirmed 9–15 Nov 2026) — but the figure rests on very high forward earnings estimates. A 10% earnings cut at constant P/E ≈ −10% on the share book. That argues for tranching, not for staying in cash.
4. **Diversify away from one country and one factor.** 13% outside the US (VEA+VWO) and 8% small-cap value (AVUV) exist because US mega-cap tech is already a huge part of VOO; without them you own one trade.
5. **Cash is a tool, not a drag.** 7% in T-bills yields ~3.7% and gives you the ability to buy during the next big drawdown instead of selling.
6. **Tax/structure matters as much as the picks.** As a HK resident you pay **30% US dividend withholding** on US-domiciled ETFs (no HK–US treaty) and US-situs assets above **USD 60,000** are exposed to **US estate tax up to 40%**. The table above is ~USD 119k US-situs, so this is a real issue — see §5.

## 4. Plain-language introduction (read this first — it stands alone)
You have HKD 1,000,000 and 10+ years. You want it to **grow**, and you have told me you can live with big ups and downs. Here is the idea in everyday words.

**Seven of the eight parts of your money own companies; the eighth holds cash-like IOUs.**
- **55% — the US market, cheaply.** Two funds buy the biggest US companies and a slice of the smaller ones. You are betting on American business in general, not trying to pick winners. This is the part that does the long-run growing.
- **25% — faster-growing bets.** Big tech (12%), small cheap companies (8%) and chipmakers (5%). These can do much better than the market — and can halve. They are capped so a disaster there costs you a few percent, not your plan.
- **13% — outside America.** Europe, Japan and emerging Asia, so you are not putting every egg in one country.
- **7% — cash-like.** Short US government IOUs paying about 3.7%. This is your shopping money for the next crash and your cushion.
- **You go in over three months, not all at once**, so one bad week does not decide your entry price. Once a year you tidy up: sell a little of whatever ran up, add to whatever lagged.

**The three risks that matter most:**
1. **The market falls.** In a bad year this could show **HKD 350,000–450,000 less**, and in a 2008-style crash **HKD 450,000–550,000 less** on paper. Historically, long-term investors have usually been rewarded for staying in — but not always, and not over every 10-year period. This is an illustration, not advice for your own money.
2. **Too much in a few tech names.** Roughly a fifth to a quarter of your money ends up in the same handful of giant technology companies. If AI spending disappoints, several parts fall together.
3. **Tax and currency.** As a Hong Kong investor you lose 30% of dividends to US withholding tax, and US holdings above USD 60,000 can face US estate tax. The HKD is pegged to the USD, so currency is stable day to day — but that also means your money is not as spread out as it looks.

## 5. Practical notes for a Hong Kong investor
- **Withholding tax:** 30% on dividends from US-domiciled ETFs. Irish-domiciled "UCITS" versions of the same funds (CSPX/VUAA for the S&P 500, CNDX for Nasdaq-100, EIMI for EM, IB01 for T-bills) pay only ~15% at fund level and sit **outside US estate tax**; they charge slightly more. A common compromise: hold the **core via UCITS** and keep only the niche US-listed satellites (AVUV, SMH) in US funds — or keep total US-situs assets under USD 60,000. **The table in §1 is the US-listed version; the UCITS core (Version B, §1) is the tax-efficient alternative.**
- **Estate tax:** US-situs assets above USD 60,000 can be taxed up to 40% for non-US persons; UCITS funds are non-US situs.
- **Currency:** no hedge needed under the peg; keep the 7% cash sleeve in USD T-bills (SGOV/IB01) or HKD deposits.
- **Costs:** blended fund fee ≈ **0.09%** (US-listed version; ≈0.13% for the UCITS version); add broker commission and FX spread (~0.05–0.3% per conversion).
- **Currency conversion plan:** convert HKD→USD in 2–3 tickets matching the tranche dates, keep undeployed cash in HKD deposits or IB01/SGOV, and never convert at a bank counter rate.
- **Pre-trade checklist:** re-check prices, yields and AUM at trade date.
