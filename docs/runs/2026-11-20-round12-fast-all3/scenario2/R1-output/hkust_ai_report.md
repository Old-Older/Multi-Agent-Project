## 1. ENGLISH WEB ARTICLE

### Headline
**Who Sets the Rules on AI? Inside HKUST's Teaching and Assessment Policy for 2025-26**

### Standfirst
HKUST gives every student and staff member free access to a homegrown AI chatbot, but the rules on using AI in coursework are written course by course — and the two campuses run different systems. Here is what is in place, and what it means for you.

### Body

**A free chatbot, and a policy that lives in the syllabus**

On 21 April 2025, HKUST gave all staff and students free access to HKGAI V1, Hong Kong's first homegrown large language model, developed by the Hong Kong Generative AI Research and Development Center. The university says it was the first local university to offer it to its whole community.

Access is not permission. HKUST imposes no single campus-wide rule on AI in coursework. Instead, each faculty member sets the generative-AI policy for their own course, and the Center for Education Innovation (CEI) requires that policy to be communicated to students in writing. So the answer to "can I use AI for this assignment?" is in your course outline — and may differ from the course next door.

**What the assessment rules say**

The Registry's "Assessment Policies and Procedures" (1 August 2025) set the frame: assessment should be transparent and criterion-referenced, and essential course information communicated clearly. The course-level AI statement is part of that information.

Some units go further. The Division of Public Policy (PPOL) states in its syllabi that, from Spring Term 2025-26, it is PPOL official policy to restrict AI use in written assignments. PPOL cites fabricated or misplaced references as commonplace forms of academic misconduct; an earlier PPOL5360 syllabus (Spring 2024-25) said that listing AI-generated references a student has not read constitutes plagiarism. Note the wording: PPOL restricts AI use in written work — it does not ban it.

**Detection: a tool, not a verdict**

CEI's guidance is cautious about AI detectors. Relying solely on detection tools "is not the best solution", the centre says — which matters if you fear a flagged draft will be treated as proof of misconduct. The emphasis is on designing assessment and stating rules clearly, not on policing text after the fact.

**AI literacy: required, but not yet for everyone**

Every undergraduate admitted from 2026-27 onward must complete six dedicated credits of AI and data literacy under a new Common Core area, HAIC (e.g. AISC 1000). Students admitted between 2022-23 and 2025-26 are not covered. For current students, AI skills are being built into the curriculum for those who follow you; your own obligations come from your programme and course outlines.

***Clear Water Bay and Guangzhou: two systems, not one**

The campuses do not share one AI setup. At HKUST(GZ), the IT office runs its own AIGC Service System in "Dialog Mode", with monthly token quotas (e.g. 500,000 ChatGPT tokens, 127,000 GPT-4 tokens, 50,000 GPT-4 Vision tokens). Students have access by default; staff must apply for API accounts, and students cannot apply directly. The service's launch date is unconfirmed in our material, so we do not date it.

HKUST(GZ) also rolls out "Major+AI" and "AI Minor" pathways from September 2026, with 450 national undergraduate places (announced 22 June 2026). A UGC Quality Assurance Council report on HKUST (August 2025) supports flexible study patterns for students physically on both campuses — many students now move between the two.

**What the research shows about students**

A peer-reviewed survey of HKUST students — He, Zhang, Anand and McMinn, *Journal of Asian Public Policy* 18(2), online 3 May 2025 — found that age, region of origin, campus and level of study significantly affect students' experience of ChatGPT. There is no single campus-wide picture: where you study and who you are shape how you meet AI.

**Faculty voice: concerns, and a training requirement**

CEI's guidance records faculty concerns about assessment design and machine-generated text. Separately, Prof. Carsten Holz's 16 September 2024 email documents a Provost's Office requirement that all faculty complete mandatory GenAI professional-development courses — one faculty member's account, not a university statement we have independently confirmed.

**What it means for you**

- **Check your course outline first.** AI rules are set course by course and must be given in writing. If a course has no written AI statement, ask for one.
- **PPOL students:** from Spring 2025-26, written assignments fall under a division policy restricting AI use. Read the syllabus before you draft.
- **A detector flag is not a verdict.** HKUST's teaching centre says detection tools alone are not the best solution.
- **Admitted from 2026-27 onward?** Six credits of AI and data literacy (HAIC, e.g. AISC 1000) are mandatory. Current students: check your programme requirements.
- **At HKUST(GZ)?** AI access runs through the campus AIGC Service System with monthly token quotas; API accounts are for staff.
- **Faculty:** state your course AI policy in writing; mandatory GenAI training has been required of all faculty.

*This report draws on HKUST's published policies and a peer-reviewed HKUST student survey; no new interviews were conducted for this piece.*

## 4. 中文版（繁體中文）

### 標題
**誰訂立AI規則？拆解科大2025-26年度教學與評核政策**

### 導言
科大讓全體師生免費使用本地研發的AI聊天機械人，但課程作業可否用AI，卻要逐科決定；清水灣與廣州校園更採用不同系統。以下整理實際已落實的安排，以及對你的影響。

### 正文

**免費工具，規則寫在課程大綱裡**

2025年4月21日，科大向全體教職員及學生免費開放HKGAI V1——由香港生成式人工智能研發中心開發、香港首個本地研發的大型語言模型。科大表示，它是首間向全校社群提供該工具的本港大學。

不過，可以使用並不等於獲准使用。科大並無就課程作業的AI使用訂立全校統一規定，而是由每位教師為自己的科目訂立生成式AI政策，並由教育創新中心（CEI）要求以書面方式通知學生。換言之，「這份功課可否用AI」的答案就在你的課程大綱裡，而且可能與隔壁科目不同。

**評核規則怎麼寫**

教務處《評核政策及程序》（2025年8月1日版本）訂下框架：評核應具透明度並以準則為本，重要課程資訊必須清楚傳達。科目的AI聲明屬於這些資訊的一部分。

部分學系走得更前。公共政策學部（PPOL）在課程大綱中列明，由2025-26年度春季學期起，限制書面作業使用AI屬學部官方政策。PPOL指出，虛構或錯置的參考文獻是常見的學術不當行為；較早的PPOL5360課程大綱（2024-25年度春季）則指出，列出學生未曾閱讀的AI生成參考文獻構成抄襲。請注意用詞：PPOL是「限制」書面作業使用AI，而非全面禁止。

**偵測工具：輔助而非判決**

CEI的指引對AI偵測工具態度審慎，指出單靠偵測工具「並非最佳方案」——這對擔心被標記即等同違規的同學尤其重要。CEI亦承認，教師對將AI融入評核、以及辨識機器生成內容有所疑慮。重點在於設計評核方式並清楚說明規則，而非事後審查文字。

**AI素養：即將必修，但暫未涵蓋所有人**

由2026-27年度起，所有於2026-27年度或之後入學的本科生，須在新的共同核心課程範疇HAIC下修讀六個學分的AI與數據素養課程（例如AISC 1000）。2022-23至2025-26年度入學的學生不受此要求涵蓋。對現屆同學而言，實際影響是：AI技能正納入其後屆別的課程，而你自身的責任則來自所屬課程及科目大綱。

**教師同樣須受訓**

教師並非置身政策之外。根據何立仁教授（Prof. Carsten Holz）2024年9月16日的教職員電郵，教務長辦公室要求全體教師完成強制性的生成式AI專業發展課程。這是個別教師對該要求的書面記錄，並非我們獨立核實的大學官方聲明。

**清水灣與廣州：兩套系統**

兩個校園並不共用同一套AI安排。在科大（廣州），資訊科技部門自行營運AIGC服務系統的「對話模式」，設有每月代幣配額（例如500,000個ChatGPT代幣、127,000個GPT-4代幣及50,000個GPT-4 Vision代幣）。學生預設可使用；教職員須申請API帳戶，學生不能直接申請。該服務的推出日期在我們手上的資料中未經確認，故不標示日期。

課程方面，科大（廣州）由2026年9月起推出「Major+AI」及「AI Minor」路徑，提供450個全國本科學額（2026年6月22日公布）。質素保證局（UGC QAC）於2025年8月發表的科大報告，支持在清水灣及廣州校園實體就讀學生採用彈性學習模式——提醒我們，不少學生現時會在兩地之間流動。

**研究顯示什麼**

一項經同行評審的科大學生調查（He、Zhang、Anand及McMinn，《Journal of Asian Public Policy》第18卷第2期，2025年5月3日網上發表）發現，年齡、來源地、校園及修讀程度，均顯著影響學生使用ChatGPT的經驗。研究並無提供全校單一圖像，而是顯示你身處何地、背景如何，會影響你在課堂上接觸AI的方式。

**對你的意義**

- **先看課程大綱。** AI規則逐科訂立，並須以書面告知。若科目沒有書面AI聲明，應主動要求。
- **PPOL同學：** 由2025-26年度春季學期起，書面作業受學部限制使用AI的政策規管，動筆前請細閱大綱用詞。
- **不要把偵測標記當作判決。** 科大教育創新中心指出，單靠偵測工具並非最佳方案。
- **2026-27年度或之後入學？** 你須修讀六個學分的AI與數據素養課程（HAIC，例如AISC 1000）。現屆同學請查閱所屬課程要求。
- **在科大（廣州）？** 你的AI使用經校園AIGC服務系統，設每月代幣配額；API帳戶供教職員申請。
- **教師：** 你須以書面說明科目的AI政策，全體教師亦須完成強制生成式AI培訓。

*本報道依據科大公開政策及一項經同行評審的科大學生調查撰寫；本文並無進行新的訪問。*
