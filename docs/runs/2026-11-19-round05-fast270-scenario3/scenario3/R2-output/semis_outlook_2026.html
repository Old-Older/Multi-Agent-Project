The Semiconductor Industry After 2026:
A Boom That Has Already Repriced

Where the cycle stands, what we would own, and what would make us wrong

Perspective date: end-2026 (market data as of 17–18 November 2026)
Prepared by: Fund Manager, with Investment Research and Risk Management
Audience: internal investment team — not for client distribution

Executive summary

Five judgments

The industry is in the largest upcycle on record — but the equity market has already paid for much of it, and the index has de-rated 34% from its June peak.

  • 1. Demand is real and still accelerating. 2026 industry revenue ≈ USD 1.51tn, +90% YoY (WSTS Spring 2026); Big-4 hyperscaler capex USD 720–745bn, +77%.
  • 2. But this is a memory-led cycle. Memory ≈ +250% to >USD 800bn — roughly four-fifths of the ~USD 715bn increase in 2026 industry revenue. Memory is the swing factor, not AI logic alone.
  • 3. The cycle is late-stage. 4Q26 DRAM contract prices +10–15% QoQ — still rising, but decelerating; NAND +15–20%.
  • 4. Valuations are not uniform. SOX forward P/E 13.6×; NVDA 16.4× vs its own 5-yr median 49.2×; AMD 61.8× vs 44.9×. Cheap leaders, expensive challengers.
  • 5. Our stance: own the bottleneck, not the beta. Overweight foundry/leading-edge and select equipment; neutral memory into 2027; underweight high-multiple challengers and China-exposed commodity capacity.
Bottom line: we stay invested but reduce index-level beta, size positions for a −30% drawdown, and pre-commit to the signals that would change our mind.

What this means

Four buyers account for the marginal dollar of leading-edge demand. Their capex is funded from operating cash flow today — but it is a discretionary number that can be cut within one quarter.

Supply response is slow

TSMC Q3 2026: revenue USD 46.72bn +41.2% YoY, gross margin 66.4%, Q4 guide USD 52.3–54.3bn, FY26 ~+44%. Management: supply gap "still quite big".

Supply

Undersupplied

Inventories historically low; capacity shifting from PC to server; PC DRAM supply could fall in 2027.

Why memory matters more than investors think

  • Memory accounts for roughly four-fifths of 2026 industry growth (WSTS: ~+250% to >USD 800bn, i.e. ~USD 570–600bn of a ~USD 715bn increase).
  • MU is the #2 weight in the SOX at 8.65% — the index is now as much a memory bet as an AI-accelerator bet.
  • Memory is the most cyclical part of semis: prices can turn within a quarter once supply catches up.

Our read

We treat 2027 memory earnings as peak-cycle, not a new base. We value memory names on mid-cycle earnings, not on 4Q26 spot pricing. Any sign of DRAM contract prices flat-to-down QoQ is our trigger to cut.

5 · Policy and geography

China: slower catch-up than feared, but a permanent policy tax

  • Export controls are tightening, not resolving. The MATCH Act cleared the House Foreign Affairs Committee (mid-Jul 2026) but is not law; it would cut China's legal supply of DUV immersion tools and servicing.
  • Rare-earth leverage is live. China's "Wave 2" suspension expires November 2026 — a near-term escalation trigger.
  • Domestic EUV is years away. Shenzhen prototype has produced EUV light but zero chips; a production-capable volume tool is no earlier than 2028–2030.
  • SMIC runs 7nm-class production via DUV multi-patterning; yields and volumes not commercially competitive with EUV rivals.
  • CXMT is reported to be scaling toward Micron-level commodity DRAM wafer capacity by end-2026 (unconfirmed, secondary source), and is ≥1 generation behind SK Hynix in HBM — a 2027–28 commodity-price risk.
  • Taiwan concentration is unchanged: TSMC remains the sole leading-edge foundry. This is the single largest un-hedgeable risk in the sector.
Investment implication: policy risk is a permanent discount on China-exposed revenue (equipment servicing, commodity memory, mature-node) and a permanent premium on non-substitutable leading-edge capacity. We do not try to trade headlines; we size for them.
Sources: Mapshock briefing 16 Jul 2026 (citing Western intelligence assessments); Disruption Banking 31 Aug 20267 / 10

7 · Key risks

What would break this thesis

1. AI capex air-pocket.

Four buyers fund USD 720–745bn of 2026 capex. A single 15–20% cut in 2027 plans would hit leading-edge utilisation and pricing within two quarters. Watch: Q3/Q4-2026 guidance, not headlines.

2. Memory rollover.

DRAM contract prices are already decelerating (+10–15% QoQ). A turn to flat-to-down would compress memory earnings and the SOX (MU = 8.65% weight).

3. Concentration.

NVDA is 6.30% of the S&P 500 (down from 8.19% on 24 Sep 2026); top-10 = 37.1%. Single-name risk re-prices fast — a live lesson from 2026.

4. Taiwan / geopolitics.

TSMC is the sole leading-edge foundry. No portfolio construction fully hedges this; only position sizing does.

5. Policy escalation.

MATCH Act progression, the November 2026 expiry of China's rare-earth suspension, and new US chip-export tariff threats were all cited in the 2026 selloff. No enacted 2026 rule change with an effective date was verified.

6. Valuation of the challengers.

AMD at 61.8× forward and 18.1× sales leaves no room for execution error; a guidance miss de-rates the whole "second source" trade.

7. Our own behaviour.

The largest realised risk in this sector is selling into a −30% drawdown. Pre-committed rules, not forecasts, are the control.
